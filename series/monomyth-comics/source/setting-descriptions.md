# Setting Descriptions

## Setting: Portal City

- First appears: Issue #1, "The Machine Who Chose the Door"
- Visual identifiers: dense modern city under apocalypse conditions, cracked asphalt, evacuation lights, emergency vehicles, ruined glass towers, smoke columns, purple portals tearing open above streets and rooftops, panicked crowds, barricades, and intermittent green light from Dyanadroid's powers.
- Continuity notes: All portal activity converges on this city. It should feel recognizably real rather than fantasy, so the impossible invaders and portals disturb an otherwise believable urban environment.
- Reference image: `../reference-images/portal-city-setting.png`

## Setting: Doctor Balmson's Emergency Lab

- First appears: Issue #1, "The Machine Who Chose the Door"
- Visual identifiers: underground or hardened emergency laboratory with flickering fluorescent lights, glass observation walls, scattered cables, portal telemetry monitors, an android deployment platform, green diagnostic lighting, and purple warning maps of the city.
- Continuity notes: Dyanadroid begins here as a created instrument rather than a citizen. The lab should visually suggest both scientific brilliance and disposability.
- Reference image: `../reference-images/doctor-balmsons-emergency-lab-setting.png`

## Setting: The Other Dimension

- First appears: Issue #1, "The Machine Who Chose the Door"
- Visual identifiers: luminous alien horizon beyond a purple threshold, unfamiliar sky geometry, impossible floating landforms, warm green reflections from Dyanadroid's power, and a quiet sense of openness after urban disaster.
- Continuity notes: Dyanadroid chooses this dimension as a self-defined future. It should feel unknown but welcoming enough to support the final moral: home is chosen, not assigned.
- Reference image: `../reference-images/other-dimension-setting.png`
