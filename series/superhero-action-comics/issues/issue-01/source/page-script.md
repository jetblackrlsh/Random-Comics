# Page Script

All pages are full realistic candid photo-comic pages in 4:5 portrait aspect ratio. Caption-box text must be generated directly inside the page art. Use caption boxes only, no speech bubbles, no thought bubbles, no floating subtitles, no blank text boxes, and no later text overlay. Every story page uses at least six panels.

## Page 1: Cover

- Story role: Introduce Mister Titanic, the rescue disaster, and the fire elemental threat.
- Layout: Single dramatic cover image with realistic photo-comic cover energy.
- Caption/title text: Cover title text: "Superhero Action Comics". Smaller issue title text: "Body of Fire".
- Visual beats: Mister Titanic bursts from smoke and flame inside a collapsing city building, his porthole eyes shining like searchlights and smoke stacks venting behind him. A humanoid fire elemental forms in the flames above the wreckage. Firefighters and civilians appear small below to show the stakes.
- Prompt notes: Realistic candid photo-comic cover, not painted poster art. Readable title text generated directly inside the cover image.

## Page 2: Into the Burning Building

- Story role: Establish the rescue stakes and Mister Titanic's physical limits inside thick smoke.
- Layout: Six panels in clear reading order.
- Caption boxes: "Mister Titanic hit the wall like a wrecking ball." "The building was already coming apart." "His metal feet carried him room to room." "Every second mattered." "He lit his eyes like searchlights." "The smoke stayed thicker than light."
- Visual beats: Mister Titanic crashes through a burning exterior wall; cracked ceiling beams sag; his heavy metal boots pound down a smoky hallway; he checks a collapsing room for survivors; his porthole eyes flare white; the final panel shows the light swallowed by black smoke.

## Page 3: Reverse the Stacks

- Story role: Show Mister Titanic adapting and clearing the way for survivors.
- Layout: Six panels, moving from problem-solving to evacuation.
- Caption boxes: "Light was not enough." "So he reversed the stacks on his back." "The room breathed inward." "Smoke vanished into his steel body." "The survivors could see again." "Mister Titanic pointed them to the way out."
- Visual beats: Close view of Mister Titanic deciding; the smoke stacks on his back rotate and pull inward; smoke funnels across the room into the stacks; survivors cough as air clears; families stand and find each other; Mister Titanic points toward the jagged hole he made in the wall.

## Page 4: Too Hot to Hold

- Story role: Show Mister Titanic's compassion and reveal the hidden villain.
- Layout: Six panels, with the final two turning from rescue to horror.
- Caption boxes: "The smallest survivor smiled through the soot." "She offered him a hug." "Mister Titanic stepped back." "His body was too hot to touch." "He stayed until the rooms were empty." "Then the fireplace came alive."
- Visual beats: A little girl smiles and opens her arms; Mister Titanic raises a gentle warning hand; heat shimmer glows from his scorched metal body; the girl shrugs and follows adults toward the exit; Mister Titanic enters a room with an old fireplace; flames twist upward into the fire elemental lunging from the hearth.

## Page 5: The Trap Has Teeth

- Story role: Reveal that the fire was a deliberate trap and escalate into superhero combat.
- Layout: Six action panels.
- Caption boxes: "The monster hit him like a furnace." "It laughed in the burning room." "This fire was bait for a hero." "Mister Titanic answered with pressure." "Smoke blasted from his stacks." "His uppercut carried them through the ceiling."
- Visual beats: The fire elemental tackles Mister Titanic to the floor; it cackles over him in flame; close panel of Mister Titanic pinned flat under burning hands; his back stacks build pressure while he is still pinned; pressurized smoke erupts downward like a jetpack blast, launching both Mister Titanic and the fire elemental upward from the floor; Mister Titanic uses the rising momentum to uppercut the fire elemental through the ceiling as both bodies rocket upward through plaster, beams, and sparks.

## Page 6: Roof of Embers

- Story role: Give the battle a violent peak, then drop Mister Titanic into the low point.
- Layout: Six panels.
- Caption boxes: "They crashed onto the roof." "Below them, the building gave way." "The fire elemental grinned." "So long, sucker." "Then it flew into the sky." "The building swallowed Mister Titanic."
- Visual beats: Mister Titanic and the fire elemental slam onto the rooftop; the roof buckles under Mister Titanic's weight; flames erupt around the villain's grin; the villain rockets away as a flaming silhouette; the building collapses inward; Mister Titanic disappears under concrete, bricks, steel, and ember dust.

## Page 7: The First Time

- Story role: Reveal Mister Titanic's origin through memory while he is trapped.
- Layout: Six panels alternating rubble-dark present with cold ocean memory.
- Caption boxes: "In the ember-dark, he remembered another ending." "He had been captain once." "He had failed passengers and crew." "As penance, he went down with the ship." "Death did not keep him." "They rebuilt him from the Titanic itself."
- Visual beats: Mister Titanic's damaged hand under rubble; memory of a captain on a tilting ocean liner deck in freezing night; lifeboats and terrified passengers in the distance; the captain standing alone as icy water rises; strange cybernetic revival lights around salvaged ship metal; Mister Titanic's cyborg body being assembled from riveted Titanic steel.

## Page 8: Not This Time

- Story role: Resolve the emotional arc and leave Mister Titanic alive for future battles.
- Layout: Six panels ending on a heroic survival image.
- Caption boxes: "The first death had come from ice." "This one came from fire." "This time, he refused to go down." "His floodlight eyes flashed through the rubble." "Firefighters saw the signal." "Mister Titanic would fight another day."
- Visual beats: Present-day Mister Titanic trapped under hot rubble; visual contrast with cold black ocean memory fading away; his hand clenches; white beams from his porthole eyes cut through dust gaps; firefighters dig with rescue tools and pull him free; final panel shows Mister Titanic sitting in ash at sunrise, damaged but alive, while firefighters and survivors look on.
