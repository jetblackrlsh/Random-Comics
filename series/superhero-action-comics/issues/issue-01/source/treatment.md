# Treatment

## Title

Body of Fire

## Issue Number

1

## Logline

Mister Titanic enters a collapsing burning building to save trapped survivors, only to discover that a fire elemental staged the disaster to bait a hero into battle.

## Central Conflict

Mister Titanic is built to save lives, but the fire elemental turns that duty into a trap. The building is not only a rescue scene; it is a battlefield designed to force Mister Titanic to choose between survival and making sure every survivor escapes.

## Theme

A hero's penance becomes meaningful only when he keeps choosing the living over his own fear of failure.

## Moral Message

Past failure can become a mission, but only if the hero refuses to surrender to guilt when the next crisis arrives.

## Ending

Buried under the collapsed building, Mister Titanic remembers the Titanic and the first time he thought he was going to die. This time, facing fire instead of ice, he refuses to go down. He flashes his floodlight eyes through the rubble until firefighters see the signal and pull him free.

## Emotional Arc

Mister Titanic begins as a relentless rescuer whose body is more machine than man, but whose mission is driven by old guilt. He clears the smoke, guides survivors out, and denies comfort because even a hug could hurt someone while his metal body is overheated. The fire elemental forces that guilt into the open by trapping him in another disaster he may not survive. Under the rubble, Mister Titanic briefly returns to the captain who failed to save his passengers and crew. His turning point is refusing to accept that old ending again. By the final page, he has not defeated the escaped villain, but he has saved the survivors and chosen to live.

## Story Shape

Eight pages: cover, smoke-blind rescue, smoke-stack reversal and survivor evacuation, refused hug and monster reveal, rooftop battle, collapse and memory of Titanic, cyborg rebirth and mission, firefighter rescue and continuing promise.
