# Treatment

## Title

The God Who Turned Away

## Issue Number

3

## Logline

Radiant Vector races into the past to preserve magic for all humanity, only to witness the fall of Atlantis and decide that unlimited power would destroy the world he hoped to save.

## Central Conflict

Radiant Vector believes the planet is unsafe because too few heroes have power. His alien supercomputer points him toward a lost magical age, but the past shows him that magic in every hand can turn civilization into a weapon. He must choose between saving one doomed city and protecting the future from the destructive power he wanted to restore.

## Theme

Power can protect a world only when responsibility limits who uses it and why.

## Moral Message

Good intentions do not make every source of power safe. Sometimes heroism means refusing a miracle after seeing what it costs.

## Ending

Radiant Vector lets ancient Atlantis sink because stopping the catastrophe would preserve a magical arms race that could consume the future. In the modern ocean, Atlantean mages remember only the myth of the sky-god who abandoned them, and they begin preparing vengeance against the hero they believe sank their city.

## Emotional Arc

Radiant Vector begins exhausted and idealistic, convinced that making everyone super would finally make Earth safe. His hope turns to horror when he sees magic used by ordinary people as casually as armies use artillery. His decisive refusal saves the future but damns him in Atlantis's memory, leaving him burdened by a choice nobody else understands.

## Story Shape

Eight pages: cover, exhausted hero and impossible wish, alien research and lost magic, speed-run into the past, the magical war of Atlantis, the city breaking away, Radiant Vector's moral choice, and the modern Atlantean vow of vengeance.
