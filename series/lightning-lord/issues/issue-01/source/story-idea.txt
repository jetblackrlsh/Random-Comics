# **Lightning Lord and the Serpent Spark**

Rain had washed York York clean sometime before dawn.

By midmorning, the city was already dirty again.

Steam rolled from vents between towers of brick, glass, and oxidized copper. Elevated trains screamed around sharp bends above six lanes of impatient traffic. Giant animated billboards crawled across the faces of buildings, competing with decades-old neon signs that nobody had bothered to take down. York York had always seemed to Lightning Lord like a city incapable of deciding what century it wanted to live in.

That was probably why magic fit into it so comfortably.

He stood at the edge of a balcony suspended several stories above a room that should not have fit inside the building containing it.

Mage Manor was like that.

From the outside, the home of the Magic Mistress appeared to be an elegant but relatively narrow stone mansion overlooking the distant York York skyline. Its architecture favored sweeping curves rather than gothic spires, with pearlescent walls broken by windows of smoky violet glass.

Inside, geometry became a suggestion.

The room beneath Lightning Lord was at least the size of a train station. Floating platforms drifted lazily through an open shaft filled with hundreds of luminous ribbons, each ribbon bearing symbols that rearranged themselves whenever he tried to focus on them. Doorways rotated through the walls without any visible mechanism. One opened briefly onto a moonlit beach before disappearing behind polished stone.

Another appeared to contain an entire library built upside down.

Lightning Lord rubbed the back of his neck.

“Okay,” he said. “I officially have no idea how big your house is.”

Behind him, the Magic Mistress smiled.

“That depends on the day.”

Unlike the stage-magician aesthetic outsiders sometimes expected from someone with her title, there was nothing theatrical about her clothing.

The Magic Mistress wore layered robes cut more like futuristic formalwear than ancient ceremonial garments. A sleeveless midnight-blue coat fell nearly to her ankles, its fabric shifting subtly between matte black and deep indigo as she moved. Thin geometric patterns of silver thread traveled across the garment like incomplete constellations.

Beneath it, she wore a fitted plum-colored tunic and flexible black trousers tucked into flat-soled boots.

Her most unusual accessory was a series of small metallic rings floating around her wrists without touching them.

Each ring was engraved with hundreds of characters from dead magical languages.

Lightning Lord had learned very quickly that when those rings started spinning, something impossible was usually about to happen.

His own appearance belonged to an entirely different world.

His charcoal jacket was cropped short enough to stay clear of the equipment strapped to his waist. Thin copper conductive lines traced the seams, feeding into compact capacitors hidden beneath reinforced shoulder panels. His shirt was pale gray, his trousers dark navy, and a pair of insulated gloves hung from his belt.

There was no cape.

No armor.

No glowing emblem announcing exactly what he could do.

The most distinctive part of his outfit was the circular device mounted between his shoulder blades—a collapsible metallic disc roughly the width of a dinner plate.

It looked unimpressive.

That was intentional.

Lightning Lord preferred equipment that didn't advertise itself.

The Magic Mistress stepped closer.

“So,” she said. “Ready?”

Lightning Lord looked at the rings circling her wrists.

“As I'm ever going to be.”

The rings began to rotate.

One clockwise.

One counterclockwise.

A third tilted vertically and spun between them.

The Magic Mistress raised her hands toward Lightning Lord without touching him.

The air changed.

It wasn't electricity.

He knew electricity.

Electricity had texture. Pressure. Direction.

This felt like standing beside an open door in a house that had previously contained no door at all.

Soft violet patterns appeared around his body.

One passed through his chest.

Another swept through his skull.

Lightning Lord resisted the instinct to step backward.

The Magic Mistress studied him.

Her expression tightened.

Then relaxed.

The floating symbols disappeared.

“Well?” Lightning Lord asked.

“I don't sense any magic in you.”

He blinked.

“None?”

“Nothing I'd call magic.”

Lightning Lord looked down at his hands.

For a moment, thin blue-white threads of electricity crawled between his fingers.

Years ago, Burst Serum had transformed Duke City.

The catastrophe had begun with a chemical release and ended with hundreds of people developing impossible abilities.

Some could liquefy their bodies.

Some grew crystalline structures from their skin.

Others manipulated heat, gravity, sound, metal, or stranger things that still resisted classification.

Lightning Lord had gained electrokinesis.

He could generate electrical current, manipulate electromagnetic fields, drain energy from powered devices, and use conductive surfaces in ways that occasionally made electrical engineers very angry.

Everything about his abilities had seemed biological.

Scientific.

At least in theory.

“So that's it?” he asked. “Burst Serum isn't magical?”

The Magic Mistress shook her head.

“I said *you* aren't magical.”

Lightning Lord looked up.

She continued.

“That tells us what happened to you. It doesn't necessarily tell us everything the substance is capable of doing.”

His disappointment vanished immediately.

“So we'd need an uncontaminated sample.”

“Preferably.”

Lightning Lord sighed.

“That's the problem.”

Most surviving Burst Serum was already inside somebody.

The original Duke City stockpiles had been confiscated, destroyed, stolen, diluted, sold, lost, or classified behind so many layers of corporate secrecy that even Lightning Lord wasn't sure which samples still existed.

But he knew one company likely had some.

PowerCore.

Lightning Lord paced toward the balcony.

“PowerCore maintained research facilities in Duke City after the incident. Their headquarters there is practically a fortress now. Too many people blame them for what happened.”

“I assume you aren't suggesting we assault a fortress.”

“No.”

He turned toward her.

“But PowerCore has a smaller laboratory here in York York.”

The Magic Mistress raised an eyebrow.

Lightning Lord smiled.

“And since I happen to be standing beside someone who apparently keeps several hundred rooms inside a building that has maybe forty windows…”

“You're wondering whether I can shorten the commute.”

“I was trying to find a polite way to ask.”

“You could have just asked.”

“Can you magically transport us to PowerCore?”

The Magic Mistress extended one hand.

The rings around her wrists accelerated.

“Hold on.”

“To what?”

Reality folded.

---

Lightning Lord's stomach remained somewhere inside Mage Manor for approximately half a second after the rest of him arrived outside PowerCore.

He staggered sideways.

The Magic Mistress caught his arm.

“You get used to it.”

“I don't think I want to.”

They had appeared beneath an elevated maglev rail several blocks from the PowerCore research center.

Lightning Lord immediately understood why the facility received less attention than its Duke City counterpart.

It barely looked important.

PowerCore had converted an old telecommunications complex into a narrow research campus wedged between a parking structure and an abandoned department store. Three interconnected rectangular buildings surrounded a central courtyard.

The company's usual branding—white circles split by a vertical crimson line—appeared only once above the entrance.

Nothing screamed *secret superhuman chemical research conducted here.*

Lightning Lord appreciated subtlety.

Then he noticed the shattered security door.

His expression changed.

“You seeing that?”

The Magic Mistress was already looking toward it.

The reinforced glass hadn't been broken inward.

Someone had torn the entire locking assembly from its housing.

Metal curled around the opening like peeled fruit skin.

Lightning Lord approached carefully.

A security camera hung from its mount above the door.

No bullet hole.

No shattered lens.

Its internal electronics had simply been crushed.

“That's not random vandalism,” he said.

“No.”

Lightning Lord crouched near the damaged lock.

There were footprints in the wet concrete.

Heavy ones.

Several people.

The Magic Mistress glanced toward the dark interior.

“PowerCore should have security.”

“Exactly.”

No alarms.

No guards.

No scientists fleeing the scene.

That bothered him more than sirens would have.

Lightning Lord straightened.

“We came for a sample.”

The Magic Mistress stepped through the ruined doorway.

“Looks like someone beat us to it.”

---

Inside, emergency lights painted the hallway amber.

Laboratory doors stood open.

Cabinets had been overturned.

A glass partition bore the impact crater of something approximately human-sized.

Lightning Lord touched two fingers to the wall.

A faint electrical pulse spread outward.

He closed his eyes.

The building became a map of current.

Backup generators.

Security circuits.

Damaged lights.

Computers.

Elevators.

A handful of emergency systems.

And movement.

“There are people ahead.”

“Employees?”

“Can't tell.”

Lightning Lord opened his eyes.

“But whoever they are, they're carrying powered equipment.”

The Magic Mistress glanced at him.

“That precise?”

“Electricity gets chatty when you know how to listen.”

They continued.

Around the next corner, they found the first unconscious security guard.

Alive.

Breathing.

Another lay near a broken desk.

No obvious wounds beyond bruising.

Lightning Lord exhaled.

“Whoever did this could've killed them.”

“But didn't.”

“Maybe.”

They moved deeper into the laboratory.

Eventually, faint voices reached them.

Lightning Lord raised one hand.

The Magic Mistress stopped.

They approached a doorway leading onto an elevated observation platform overlooking a large chemical storage room.

Below them stood five men.

Their clothing immediately caught the Magic Mistress's attention.

“Bulk Brute.”

Lightning Lord glanced toward her.

“You recognize them?”

“Their gear.”

The men didn't wear uniforms so much as pieces of industrial machinery converted into clothing.

Heavy gray trousers.

Steel-toed boots.

Sleeveless impact vests reinforced with thick ceramic plates.

Their faces were covered by angular respirator masks whose segmented filters created blunt, almost bulldog-like silhouettes.

Most distinctive were the mechanical harnesses fitted across their backs.

Each harness contained transparent tubing connected to a central cylindrical reservoir filled with an iridescent green fluid.

Lightning Lord whispered.

“Viper.”

The Magic Mistress nodded.

Bulk Brute's organization had spent years distributing Viper through York York's criminal underground.

The chemical temporarily increased muscle density, reflex speed, pain tolerance, and metabolic output.

But Viper's effects went beyond performance enhancement.

Repeated use caused reptilian mutations.

Skin hardened into scale-like plates.

Pupils narrowed vertically.

Teeth lengthened.

The body became broader, denser, and increasingly inhuman.

Bulk Brute himself had used more Viper than anyone alive.

Rumors claimed his body could no longer function without it.

Lightning Lord stared at the nearest man's harness.

Then frowned.

“I've seen photos of Viper rigs.”

“So?”

“They don't look like that.”

The Magic Mistress studied the equipment.

Then saw it.

Each harness contained two reservoir ports.

One already held Viper.

The second was empty.

“Why would they need two?” she whispered.

Below them, one of the men opened a reinforced storage case.

Cold vapor spilled over its edges.

Inside were several narrow glass ampoules.

The liquid within them glowed faintly blue.

Lightning Lord's breath caught.

“No.”

The Magic Mistress looked at him.

“That's—”

“Burst Serum.”

The henchman removed one ampoule.

Lightning Lord vaulted over the observation rail.

“DON'T!”

The man looked up.

Then smiled behind his respirator.

He slammed the ampoule into the empty port.

Viper on the left.

Burst Serum on the right.

Both reservoirs unlocked simultaneously.

The harness activated.

The henchman's body convulsed.

Green fluid shot through one network of tubes.

Blue fluid shot through another.

The two substances met inside the mechanical regulator at the center of his spine.

For one second, nothing happened.

Then the room exploded.

Not with fire.

With force.

A pressure wave ripped outward from the man.

Lightning Lord was thrown across the chamber.

The Magic Mistress disappeared behind a curtain of shattered glass.

Storage racks collapsed.

Metal cabinets skidded across the floor.

Every light in the room burst simultaneously.

Darkness followed.

For several seconds, Lightning Lord heard nothing but ringing.

He pushed himself upright.

“Magic Mistress?”

“I'm here.”

A silver glow appeared across the room.

The Magic Mistress stood slowly, one hand pressed against a cut above her eyebrow.

The rings around her wrists illuminated the ruined laboratory.

Then she froze.

The henchman stood at the center of the wreckage.

He had changed.

Viper had expanded his body, adding thick overlapping scales along his shoulders and forearms.

But the second mutation was unlike anything Lightning Lord had seen in Duke City.

Symbols moved beneath the man's skin.

Not tattoos.

Not veins.

Symbols.

They glowed gold, appearing and disappearing faster than Lightning Lord could identify them.

The Magic Mistress stared.

Lightning Lord noticed.

“What?”

Her rings were rotating on their own.

“I can feel him.”

“What does that mean?”

“Magic.”

Lightning Lord looked from her to the transformed henchman.

“You said you couldn't detect magic in me.”

“I can't.”

“But Burst Serum—”

“I know.”

The henchman laughed.

It came out as a low hiss.

The Magic Mistress stepped closer, fascinated despite the danger.

“There are people born with a latent affinity for magic,” she said. “Different cultures have different names for them. Modern scholars usually call them Magi Manis.”

Lightning Lord kept his eyes on the henchman.

“So he's one?”

“Maybe not originally.”

That got Lightning Lord's attention.

The Magic Mistress continued.

“Your Burst Serum altered your biology enough to create electrokinesis. If its mutations aren't limited to conventional biological systems…”

Her eyes widened.

“…it may have altered *his* capacity for magic.”

Lightning Lord slowly turned toward the glowing man.

“So Burst Serum can turn someone into a Magi Manis.”

The Magic Mistress swallowed.

“I think it just did.”

The henchman pulled the storage case toward himself.

Several remaining ampoules rattled inside.

“Bulk Brute is going to love this.”

Lightning Lord stepped forward.

“Yeah. That's not leaving this building.”

The henchman charged.

The Magic Mistress reacted first.

Her wrist rings separated.

Silver threads erupted between them.

“BOUND AND STILL!”

The threads snapped across the laboratory and wrapped around the henchman's limbs.

For half a second, they held.

Then the man's jaw opened.

His tongue had changed.

Long.

Black.

Forked.

He hissed something.

Not English.

Not any language Lightning Lord knew.

The sound made the room feel colder.

Golden symbols flashed across the henchman's throat.

The Magic Mistress's bindings shattered.

She stumbled.

“What happened?” Lightning Lord shouted.

The henchman barreled between them.

Lightning Lord fired electricity toward the floor ahead of him, attempting to overload the man's harness.

The transformed criminal leaped clear across the room.

He smashed through an emergency exit.

“Why didn't your spell work?”

“No idea!”

They ran.

Outside, the henchman sprinted through the industrial district at impossible speed.

His Viper-enhanced legs struck the pavement hard enough to crack it.

Lightning Lord ripped the metal disc from his back.

It unfolded in midair.

Segmented panels expanded outward until the device became a broad, circular platform roughly five feet across.

Lightning Lord thrust both hands toward it.

Electromagnetic energy surged.

The disc lifted.

He jumped onto it.

“Need a ride?”

The Magic Mistress stepped aboard.

The disc accelerated.

Wind slammed into them.

Ahead, the henchman raced through traffic, bounding over cars and vaulting concrete barriers without slowing.

Lightning Lord poured more electricity into the disc.

Blue-white arcs crawled across its rim.

Still, they barely gained ground.

“So,” Lightning Lord shouted over the wind, “about the snake language!”

“Serpent Tongue!”

“Yeah! That!”

The Magic Mistress held one hand against Lightning Lord's shoulder to steady herself.

“Magic develops in layers. Traditions build on older traditions.”

“And?”

“And older magic tends to have deeper roots!”

Lightning Lord swerved around a delivery drone.

“You're giving me the textbook version!”

“The SparkNotes version is that the older a form of magic is, the harder it is for newer magic to override it!”

The henchman glanced backward.

His forked tongue flickered between his fangs.

Lightning Lord grimaced.

“How old is Serpent Tongue?”

The Magic Mistress hesitated.

Lightning Lord looked at her.

“That pause was too long.”

“Some traditions trace it back to humanity's earliest myths.”

“How early?”

“Garden-of-Eden early.”

Lightning Lord nearly lost his concentration.

“Oh, come on!”

A coil of golden magical energy snapped backward from the fleeing henchman.

Lightning Lord banked sharply.

The spell missed them and sliced a streetlight cleanly in half.

“So you're telling me he's using prehistoric snake magic?!”

“I'm telling you we should discuss religious anthropology after we stop him!”

“Fair!”

The henchman accelerated.

Lightning Lord gritted his teeth.

The disc's systems were reaching their thermal limits.

“I can't catch him.”

“We have to.”

“If he gets that Burst Serum back to Bulk Brute…”

The Magic Mistress looked toward the case under the henchman's arm.

“One Viper-powered criminal with unpredictable magic is a problem.”

Lightning Lord understood.

“An army of them takes York York.”

They fell silent.

Think.

Lightning Lord stared at the man's back.

The harness.

Green reservoir.

Blue reservoir.

Pumps.

Regulator.

Tubes.

Power indicators.

Electricity.

His eyes widened.

“The rig.”

“What about it?”

“Viper doesn't permanently make them like that, right?”

“No. Bulk Brute's people need regular doses.”

“And the rig pumps it into them.”

“Yes.”

“What powers the rig?”

The Magic Mistress looked at him.

Then smiled.

“Oh.”

Lightning Lord crouched low over the floating disc.

“Exactly.”

He extended one hand.

Not toward the man.

Toward the harness.

Lightning Lord reached with his senses.

There.

A lithium power cell.

Small.

Shielded.

But electrical.

“Found you.”

The henchman stumbled.

A spark jumped from the harness.

Then another.

Electrical energy streamed backward through the air in thin ribbons.

Lightning Lord pulled.

The Viper rig's charge drained.

Warning lights flashed.

The pumps slowed.

The henchman looked over his shoulder.

“What are you doing?!”

Lightning Lord smiled.

“Making you eco-friendly.”

He pulled harder.

The rig died.

The effect was immediate.

The henchman slowed.

His stride shortened.

Scales loosened from his skin and dissolved into flakes of green-gray material.

His shoulders contracted.

The golden symbols beneath his skin flickered.

The Magic Mistress's eyes widened.

“The magic is disappearing.”

Lightning Lord looked at her.

“Seriously?”

“His Magi Manis transformation must be temporary.”

“So Burst Serum mixed with Viper behaves differently than Burst Serum normally does.”

“Apparently.”

Lightning Lord drained the final remnants of energy from the rig.

The henchman collapsed to his knees.

His forked tongue shortened.

His fangs receded.

The symbols vanished.

The Magic Mistress stepped to the edge of the disc.

“My turn.”

Her rings shot outward.

This time, no Serpent Tongue answered her.

Silver chains erupted from circles of light surrounding the henchman.

They wrapped around his arms.

His legs.

His torso.

He slammed into the pavement.

The chains locked into glowing geometric seals.

The Magic Mistress landed beside him.

“And stay there.”

Lightning Lord brought the disc down nearby.

He stepped off.

For several seconds, neither hero spoke.

Then Lightning Lord noticed something.

“The case.”

It had broken open during the fall.

The remaining Burst Serum ampoules had scattered across the roadside.

Most were shattered.

Rainwater flowed through glowing blue liquid, carrying it toward a muddy drainage ditch.

Lightning Lord hurried over.

“No, no, no…”

He lifted one intact-looking ampoule.

A crack ran through its side.

Mud had already entered.

Another was completely empty.

Another had been crushed beneath the henchman's boot.

The Magic Mistress joined him.

“Any usable?”

Lightning Lord checked them one by one.

“No.”

The final ampoule slipped apart between his fingers.

He stared at the contaminated serum swirling through the mud.

They had stopped Bulk Brute's people.

They had saved York York from whatever nightmare an entire gang of Viper-powered magical mutants might have become.

But the reason they had come to PowerCore in the first place was gone.

Lightning Lord laughed once.

The Magic Mistress looked at him.

“What's funny?”

“I came to York York because I needed a clean Burst Serum sample.”

She looked at the glowing mud.

“Yes.”

“And now the only clean samples we knew about are literally in a gutter.”

“Yes.”

He sighed.

“Fantastic.”

The Magic Mistress looked back toward the unconscious henchman.

“Not entirely.”

Lightning Lord followed her gaze.

She continued.

“We learned Burst Serum can interact with magic.”

“We learned it can apparently create Magi Manis.”

“Temporarily, under these conditions.”

“And Viper changes the outcome.”

“And we learned Bulk Brute wants the serum.”

Lightning Lord's humor disappeared.

Somewhere in York York, someone had designed a dual-injection rig capable of combining Viper with Burst Serum.

That wasn't improvisation.

That was engineering.

Planning.

Testing, perhaps.

Someone had known enough about both chemicals to believe the combination was worth attempting.

Lightning Lord folded his disc and returned it to his back.

“So this wasn't just a robbery.”

“I doubt it.”

“And if Bulk Brute knows what Burst Serum can do…”

“He'll try again.”

Lightning Lord looked toward the distant skyline.

York York stretched around them beneath low clouds, its towers fading into gray.

He had come looking for an answer to something that happened in Duke City.

Instead, Duke City's past had followed him here.

Magic Mistress stepped beside him.

“Still interested in figuring out what Burst Serum really is?”

Lightning Lord glanced toward the silver rings floating around her wrists.

Then toward the powerless Viper rig lying beside the captured henchman.

Science.

Magic.

Two systems that should have remained separate.

Two systems Burst Serum had just forced together.

He smiled.

“More than ever.”

Behind them, sirens began to approach.

Ahead of them waited Bulk Brute.

And somewhere between those two points was the answer to a question neither science nor sorcery could solve alone.

Lightning Lord extended his hand.

“Partners?”

The Magic Mistress looked at it.

Then shook it.

“Until we figure this out.”

Lightning Lord nodded toward the city.

“Something tells me that's going to take a while.”

Above them, thunder rolled through the clouds.

For once, Lightning Lord had nothing to do with it.

**END**
