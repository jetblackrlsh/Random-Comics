Series continuity: Issue #3 ended with a corrupted four-armed bandit emerging from the rocks after the Chronicler learned about false hunger. She is an intelligent person, not a beast, but her corrupted body is huge, unstable, and strong enough to kill someone if she reaches the team.

Issue #4 premise: The entire issue is the bandit encounter. The Guardians begin by backing away through jagged crystal terrain while the bandit advances slowly, asking for residue because the corruption has convinced her she is starving. The Chronicler refuses to treat her like a beast, but mercy makes the tactical problem harder: they cannot let her touch them, cannot kill her if there is still a chance to save her, and cannot teleport her blindly to the gate if the Arcanum Guard will execute her on sight.

The bandit must be both pitiable and physically terrifying. She is not fast, but she does not need to be. Her four massively muscular arms, corrupted spasms, and unstable motor control turn every step into pressure. The key strength reveal is a missed punch that obliterates a natural crystal wall, proving that if she reaches the team, one hit could end the fight.

The resolution should use teamwork under time pressure: Quasar tries to contact the gate guards through corrupted signal interference; Vessara throws catalysts into the air; the Chronicler teleports the catalysts onto the bandit so they can make contact without Vessara getting close; Kaelen overextends void-tech to stabilize the bandit's corrupted energy field; Quasar confirms the guards are ready with containment; the Chronicler teleports the team behind the bandit to escape the corner, then teleports the bandit to containment at the gate. The bandit survives, but Kaelen's suit is damaged by the overextension, forcing the next move toward a sanctuary shelter for repairs.
