# Treatment

## Title

No Time To Rest

## Issue Number

3

## Logline

After the first anomaly cleansing, the Chronicler learns why the exhausted Wild Zone Guardians cannot simply rest, and a starving corrupted bandit turns the lesson into a cliffhanger.

## Central Conflict

The Chronicler wants to understand what happens after a mission, expecting recovery, repairs, and a clean return to safety. Kaelen and Vessara reveal that Guardian work is a cycle of reporting, documenting, rationing, stimulants, and moving again before corruption patterns shift. The larger conflict is whether the Chronicler can still treat the Wild Zones as adventure material once the consequences include sleep deprivation, contaminated food, and intelligent people losing themselves to false hunger.

## Theme

Hero work does not end when the monster falls; the cost continues in exhausted bodies, damaged land, and victims who are harder to fight than beasts.

## Ending

The issue ends on the first appearance of a corrupted four-armed bandit. She asks for residue because the corruption has convinced her she is starving. The Guardians realize she is both a person in need of containment and a physical powerhouse who can kill them if she gets close. The last image leaves the team backing away as the bandit advances through the crystal field.

## Emotional Arc

The Chronicler begins with post-battle curiosity: what do real heroes do after the action sequence? The answers turn the victory sour. Kaelen and Vessara's exhaustion, reliance on stimulants, and knowledge of corruption's effects make the Chronicler more protective of the team and more uneasy about turning their suffering into comics. By the final page, the Chronicler understands that the next "enemy" is a person who needs saving, and that compassion may make the situation more dangerous.

## Story Shape

Eight pages: cover, the team standing in the cleansed anomaly site, the practical aftermath of reporting and residue, the Guardians' lack of rest, the stimulant-and-ration reality of field work, corruption spreading through plants and beasts, the horror of intelligent infection and false hunger, and the corrupted four-armed bandit emerging as the cliffhanger.

## Caption Approach

Use story-clarity captions with 3-4 large panels on most pages. This issue is lore-heavy, but the captions should stay grounded in visible aftermath: tired posture, diagnostic displays, ration packs, maps, corrupted vegetation, and Quasar's medical-style scans. The final two pages should shift from explanation into dread, using fewer panels and more empty space as the bandit approaches.
