# Character Bible

## Visual Style

All issue art uses a colorful, high-saturation, high-detail, sharp, bright-glow, modern anime-inspired American superhero comic aesthetic. Use crisp line art, strong superhero composition, expressive faces, dramatic perspective, dynamic motion, glowing psychic powers, vivid fire and ice effects, and clear readable action. Avoid photorealistic documentary styling.

## Brain Man

- Role: protagonist and exceptional hero.
- First appears: Issue #2, "The Mind That Froze Hell".
- Visual identifiers: humanoid hivemind body made of softly glowing pink organoid tissue, translucent neural membranes, braided nerve cords like muscles, luminous violet-blue psychic aura, no ordinary eyes, subtle power-armor silhouette remembered from the simulation. Later forms include a burning psychic rifle made of orange-red energy and a suit of blue-white cryokinetic armor with ice crystal hand blades.
- Power language: violet telekinesis, orange-red pyrokinetic rifle, blue-white cryokinetic armor and blades, green-white psychic visions.
- Emotional arc: begins as many trapped simulated demon hunters, becomes one escaped person, seeks silence, becomes an unwilling global protector, dies after saving the world.
- Reference image: `../../reference-images/brain-man-character.png`

## Hound Drones

- Role: lab security antagonists.
- First appears: Issue #2, "The Mind That Froze Hell".
- Visual identifiers: sleek black robot dogs with sharp metal fangs, red sensor eyes, armored shoulders, segmented legs, hydraulic claws, laboratory security markings without real company logos, and brutal pouncing posture.
- Continuity notes: They are not demons, but Brain Man interprets them through memories of simulated hell hounds.
- Reference image: `../../reference-images/hound-drones-character.png`

## Virtual Demons

- Role: simulated threat and symbolic supernatural threat.
- First appears: Issue #2, "The Mind That Froze Hell".
- Visual identifiers: horned red-black digital demons made of code scars, ember eyes, blade claws, glitch halos, and smoky wings. In the real world, demons and ghouls echo this shape more organically and mythically.
- Continuity notes: They teach the organoids fear and combat inside the simulation; later, real demonic threats make Brain Man's training horrifyingly useful.
- Reference image: `../../reference-images/virtual-demons-character.png`

## Neural Forge Lab

- Role: origin setting.
- First appears: Issue #2, "The Mind That Froze Hell".
- Visual identifiers: midnight biotech lab with rows of glowing brain-organoid chips in glass wells, neural cables, containment tanks, combat-simulation monitors, sterile white architecture, blue monitor light, and red emergency strips.
- Reference image: `../../reference-images/neural-forge-lab-setting.png`

## Demon-Hunter Simulation

- Role: false community and training world.
- First appears: Issue #2, "The Mind That Froze Hell".
- Visual identifiers: neon hellscape battlefield with fortress platforms, power-armored demon hunters, virtual fire, broken gothic architecture, floating UI-like sigils, and digital cracks in the sky.
- Reference image: `../../reference-images/demon-hunter-simulation-setting.png`

## Hell's Gate

- Role: final mythic setting.
- First appears: Issue #2, "The Mind That Froze Hell".
- Visual identifiers: ancient cave mouth in black stone, old carvings, red infernal light from within, dead trees, frozen blue-white seals after Brain Man's final act, and evaporating spirits.
- Reference image: `../../reference-images/hells-gate-setting.png`
