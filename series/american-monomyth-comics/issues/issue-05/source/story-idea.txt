Create the next issue of American Monomyth Comics from this premise:

The land of trolls is a utopia of green grass, pink flowers, rivers that flow uphill, and massive mountains. The trolls have shaggy fur, bell-shaped heads, horns, and red eyes. Elite troll climbers train constantly to be the best mountain climbers in the world.

Mount Master, the largest mountain in the land, is home to The Master, a giant ghostly white-furred god of the trolls. The Master wields the Master Rod, a lightning-powered staff that contains his power. The Master promised a race to the top of Mount Master, and the winner would claim the Master Rod.

Seven elite climbers, one of each color, are expected to compete. Purple Climber has unnatural magical speed fueled by purple lightning. She is greedy, and her powers give her an unfair advantage, so the utopia fears what will happen if she becomes the goddess of the trolls.

A wandering hero enters the land, hears the fear, and decides to stop Purple Climber from claiming the rod. The hero visits Purple Climber and invites her to a greater race in a faraway village. Purple is reluctant because she might miss the Master Rod race, but the hero claims the faraway race has a greater prize. Purple's greed wins, and she follows the hero away on a winding path to nowhere.

As they travel, the hero learns Purple became greedy because her parents died young after giving away too much food and too many belongings. Purple became greedy because she feared sharing would destroy her. The hero teaches her that wanting things is allowed and helping others is also allowed, but either extreme can ruin a life.

Purple accepts the lesson. The hero leads her back to troll land just as the Master Rod race begins. Purple races up Mount Master, wins the rod, and terrifies the trolls. She proves she has changed by using the Master Rod to help the land instead of hoarding power. She searches for the hero, but the hero is already gone.

Purple honors the hero with a statue. Generations pass, the Master Rod is passed to other trolls, and the hero's statue crumbles until the hero is forgotten. The price of peace is that the memory of heroes becomes lost to time. It is a price worth paying.
