# The Dragon Blade of Moon Lantern Temple

## Issue Number

6

## Title

The Dragon Blade of Moon Lantern Temple

## Logline

When a dropped ceremony candle burns away the seal that imprisoned clawed spirits beneath a sacred rabbit temple, wandering red-armored samurai Akaryu calls on the Dragon God, destroys the spirits with golden blade-light, saves Tsukihana Village, and leaves before gratitude can make him stay.

## Theme

Peace is worth protecting even when the protector must be forgotten after the danger ends.

## Moral Tension

Kiko's accident releases the spirits, but the story does not blame a child for evil sealed long before she was born. The village must survive without turning fear into shame, and Akaryu must save them without making the village depend on him forever.

## Hero Desire

Akaryu wants Tsukihana Village to return to peaceful ordinary life, with the temple sealed and the child forgiven.

## Hero Burden

Akaryu carries dragon-blessed power that can strike incorporeal spirits, but that power keeps him moving from town to town. If he settles as one village's honored guardian, other helpless communities will face supernatural danger alone.

## Threatened Community

Tsukihana Village is a feudal-Japan rabbit utopia of lantern streets, rice terraces, arched bridges, moon-viewing gardens, candle ceremonies, and gentle families who honor Moon Lantern Temple because it protects their peace.

## Mythic Evil

The Ashen Claw Spirits are dangerous incorporeal spirits imprisoned behind the temple's Spirit Seal. When the seal burns away, they leap into the town, slash through charms and prayer beads, ignore spears, and feed on fear.

## Why Institutions Fail

The Candle-Keeper Monks know the prayers and beads that preserve peace, but the spirits slice the beads apart before the prayers can bind them. The Moon Gate Guards are brave and disciplined, but their spears pass through the spirits' smoke bodies without harming them.

## Hero's Exceptional Quality

Akaryu is a wandering humanoid rabbit samurai in red dragon armor. He does not merely have courage; he has a Dragonlit Katana that can receive the Dragon God's golden light and cut spirits that ordinary weapons cannot touch.

## Redemptive Action

Akaryu calmly walks toward the spirits while they mock him, calls on the Dragon God without anger, accepts the golden light through his blade, and uses precise acrobatic sword strokes to destroy the spirits without harming the villagers or the temple.

## Restoration

The Dragon God's light reseals Moon Lantern Temple, the fires go out, the monks gather Kiko safely, the guards help rebuild, and Tsukihana Village turns relief into forgiveness instead of blame.

## Why the Hero Cannot Join

The villagers ask Akaryu to remain as their permanent protector, but he knows the same darkness can break seals in other towns. Staying would comfort Tsukihana while abandoning the road that needs him.

## Final Withdrawal Image

Akaryu leaves under dawn lanterns. Generations later, his statue cracks and crumbles beside a peaceful ceremony where children light candles safely, not knowing his name because the peace he restored lasted.

## 10-Step American Monomyth Outline

### Pages 2-3: The Edenic Community

Tsukihana Village is introduced as a beautiful rabbit utopia in feudal Japan, preparing for its candle lighting ceremony. Families, monks, guards, and children all trust Moon Lantern Temple because its sacred Spirit Seal has protected them for generations.

### Pages 4-5: Evil Threatens the Community

Kiko trips during the ceremony and drops her candle on the temple steps. Flames crawl into the Spirit Seal, burn away the protective mark, and the Ashen Claw Spirits burst free from the temple to attack the village.

### Pages 6-7: Ordinary Institutions Fail

The Candle-Keeper Monks charge forward with prayer beads, but the spirits slice the beads apart with claws. The Moon Gate Guards thrust spears into the spirits, but the spearheads pass through their incorporeal bodies and leave the town defenseless.

### Pages 8-9: The Community Becomes Helpless

Families flee through lantern streets, monks shield children with broken beads, guards form useless lines, and Kiko realizes her accident opened the danger. Everyone understands that ordinary courage cannot touch the spirits.

### Pages 10-11: The Exceptional Hero Appears

Akaryu, a wandering rabbit samurai in red dragon armor, arrives at the burning temple gate. He sees the broken seal, the useless weapons, and the terrified villagers, then calmly draws the Dragonlit Katana because this is exactly why he wanders.

### Pages 12-13: The Hero Resists Ordinary Limitations

The spirits mock Akaryu for carrying a physical sword against smoke bodies, but he keeps walking. He calls on the Dragon God, golden light pours through the blade, and the spirits realize this sword can reach them.

### Pages 14-15: Redemptive Force Confronts Evil

Akaryu launches into acrobatic swordwork, slicing through spirits with golden dragon-light while protecting the villagers behind him. His victory comes from disciplined courage joined to sacred service, not rage or pride.

### Pages 16-17: The Community Is Restored

The last spirits dissolve, the golden light seals Moon Lantern Temple again, the fires die, and the village gathers around Kiko with forgiveness. Tsukihana begins rebuilding its steps and lantern path.

### Pages 18-19: The Hero Does Not Fully Join the Community

The villagers build a statue and invite Akaryu to stay as their honored guardian. He accepts their gratitude but refuses the permanent place because other villages across the land still need a wandering protector.

### Pages 20-21: The Hero Withdraws or Waits

Akaryu leaves at dawn. Generations pass, the statue crumbles, children light candles safely, and the hero is forgotten because peace has become normal again.

### Page 22: Poster-Style Back Cover Summary

The back cover frames Akaryu with the golden Dragonlit Katana before Moon Lantern Temple, Kiko's fallen candle and the broken seal below, fleeing spirits above, and the later crumbling statue at the bottom, turning the issue into a bright poster about protection, forgiveness, and a hero who leaves so peace can last.
