# The Equal World Split

## Issue Number

13

## Logline

When immortal dark elf Lord Veyr brainwashes Equalia's humans for refusing to worship him, orphan troll Grum rejects the excuse of a better alternate universe, uses the Universe Splitter to choose rescue in his own world, and restores a city that honors him by treating him like everyone else.

## Core Premise

Equalia is a human utopia on a new world where no citizen receives special treatment. The custom is simple and visible: every person is greeted with respect, helped when in need, judged by the same laws, and invited to the same tables. When a hungry orphan troll named Grum reaches the city gates, the humans do not treat him as a monster, mascot, servant, noble guest, or curiosity. They feed him, teach him, house him, and raise him with the same kindness they give every child.

Grum grows into a towering adult troll whose strength could make him exceptional in many societies. Equalia refuses to turn him into a symbol or weapon. He works, studies, laughs, helps neighbors, and receives neither fear nor special privilege. That ordinary equality becomes the treasure he loves most.

Lord Veyr, an immortal dark elf sorcerer, arrives in Equalia expecting worship. He believes long life and vast magic prove he deserves obedience. The citizens refuse without hatred. They explain that Equalia treats everyone with equal dignity, so even an immortal mage may be welcomed as a person but not worshipped as a superior being.

Veyr is insulted by equal respect because it denies the hierarchy he wants. He casts a domination spell across the city. It brainwashes every human into kneeling obedience, but the spell only works on humans. Grum remains free and confronts Veyr. Grum is physically stronger, but Veyr uses a Universe Splitter device to turn the conflict into a philosophical trap.

The Universe Splitter divides reality according to chosen options. Veyr sets one option where he surrenders and retreats, and another where he refuses. The split resolves for this issue's world as the branch where Veyr refuses. He tells Grum to be satisfied because another universe now exists where Equalia was saved without violence.

Grum briefly struggles with the idea. The result he wants exists somewhere, but not for the people standing in front of him. He refuses to abandon his own world just because a better branch exists elsewhere. He grabs the Universe Splitter and creates two new branches: one where he banishes himself in shame, and one where he uppercuts Veyr to the other side of the planet. This world resolves as the branch where Grum fights. Veyr flies so far from Equalia that his brainwashing spell breaks.

The humans awaken. They ask what happened to the branch where Grum left in shame. Grum answers that he probably went beyond the city to find a hero's guild, because the lesson of the Universe Splitter is not surrender to alternate outcomes. The lesson is that there is always a way to keep trying to save the day.

Equalia returns to normal. The citizens do not build statues, declare Grum king, or make him a separate class of hero. They thank him by treating him exactly as they always did: as their equal neighbor. That is precisely the victory Grum wanted.

## Threatened Community

The Edenic community is Equalia, a human utopia of open gates, shared tables, public gardens, transparent civic halls, equal schools, and ordinary kindness. It is worth saving because its people live the principle that dignity does not depend on power, species, age, wealth, magic, or status.

## Mythic Evil

The mythic evil is Lord Veyr's demand for worship backed by immortal magic. His brainwashing spell attacks Equalia's central value by turning equal citizens into obedient subjects.

## Why Institutions Fail

Equalia's council, teachers, healers, guards, and civic systems are humane and competent, but they are run by humans. Veyr's spell directly controls human minds, so every ordinary institution is captured at the same moment. Equalia fails not because it is foolish, but because the attack bypasses normal courage and law.

## Hero's Exceptional Quality

Grum is exceptional because he is not human, so the domination spell cannot touch his mind. He is also stronger than Veyr, but his true heroic quality is moral loyalty to his own world. He refuses to treat an alternate happy branch as a substitute for saving the people beside him.

## Redemptive Action

Grum takes the Universe Splitter from Veyr and makes a clear active choice: he will not disappear in shame while Equalia remains enslaved. He creates a branch where he fights for this world, and this story follows that branch. His uppercut is redemptive because it rejects both tyranny and passive multiverse excuses.

## Restoration

Veyr lands far beyond the city, too distant for his spell to hold the human minds. Equalia's citizens awaken, remember their value, and return to equal daily life without turning Grum into a superior class of person.

## Final Separation

Grum does not withdraw by leaving Equalia. He withdraws from hero worship. The community remains whole because it refuses to separate him into a monument, ruler, celebrity, or sacred exception. He stays present as an equal neighbor.

## Theme

Equal dignity means refusing both tyrants who demand worship and victories that would make the rescuer above everyone else.

## Moral Tension

The Universe Splitter creates a real better world somewhere else, so Grum must decide whether that is enough. His answer is practical and moral: a saved alternate branch does not excuse abandoning the enslaved branch where he still stands.

## Hero Desire

Grum wants Equalia's people free, Equalia's equal respect restored, and his own place in the city preserved as ordinary belonging rather than special treatment.

## Hero Burden

Grum is the only person free enough to act, which risks making him the one unequal person in a city built against special status. After victory, he must help the city return to equality without letting gratitude become worship.

## Final Withdrawal Image

Grum walks through Equalia carrying groceries with neighbors, smiling because no statue rises behind him, no crowd kneels, and every citizen greets him with the same warm respect they give one another.

## 10-Step American Monomyth Outline

### Step 01: The Edenic Community

Pages 2-3 establish Equalia's equal kindness and show Grum being accepted as an orphan child, then growing up without fear or special treatment. A dark magical arrival appears at the gate.

### Step 02: Evil Threatens the Community

Pages 4-5 reveal Lord Veyr demanding worship and brainwashing the humans when Equalia refuses. Grum remains unaffected because the spell only works on humans.

### Step 03: Ordinary Institutions Fail

Pages 6-7 show Equalia's council, guards, teachers, healers, and civic systems falling under the same spell. Grum realizes every normal human response has been captured.

### Step 04: The Community Becomes Helpless

Pages 8-9 show the humans kneeling under Veyr's control while Grum sees his family, teachers, and neighbors enslaved. He steps forward because no one else can.

### Step 05: The Exceptional Hero Appears

Pages 10-11 show adult Grum confronting Veyr and demanding the spell's end. Veyr reveals the Universe Splitter as a clever answer to Grum's strength.

### Step 06: The Hero Resists Ordinary Limitations

Pages 12-13 show Veyr splitting reality into surrender and refusal branches, with this world landing in the refusal branch. Grum rejects the idea that another saved universe makes this enslaved one acceptable.

### Step 07: Redemptive Force Confronts Evil

Pages 14-15 show Grum seizing the Universe Splitter, creating his own pair of choices, and making this branch the one where he uppercuts Veyr across the planet.

### Step 08: The Community Is Restored

Pages 16-17 show Veyr too far away to control Equalia, the humans awakening, and the citizens asking what happened in the branch where Grum left.

### Step 09: The Hero Does Not Fully Join the Community

Pages 18-19 show Grum explaining that the shame-branch version of himself probably sought help elsewhere, and the city deciding not to turn him into a superior hero.

### Step 10: The Hero Withdraws or Waits

Pages 20-21 show Equalia returning to ordinary equal kindness. Grum remains in the city but withdraws from special status, content to be treated like everyone else.

## Back Cover Concept

The back cover is a poster-style summary: Grum stands between Equalia's warm civic light and Veyr's dark magic, holding the glowing Universe Splitter as branching realities spiral behind him. One branch shows Veyr fleeing, one shows Grum leaving for help, and the central branch shows Equalia restored. Integrated poster caption text: "SAVE THE WORLD YOU STAND IN." "EQUALITY NEEDS NO WORSHIP." "THE HERO ASKED FOR NO MONUMENT."
