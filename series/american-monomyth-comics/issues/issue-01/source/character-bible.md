# Character Bible

## Visual Style

All issue art uses a colorful, high-saturation, high-detail, sharp, bright-glow, modern anime-inspired American superhero comic aesthetic. Use crisp line art, strong superhero anatomy, expressive anime faces, dramatic perspective, dynamic motion, glowing powers, vivid energy effects, and clear readable action. Avoid photorealistic documentary styling.

## Datacenter Giants

- Role: collective protagonist and exceptional hero.
- First appears: Issue #1, "The Invisible Giants".
- Monstrous form: massive server-campus creatures with cooling-tower horns, cable-tendril arms, concrete armor, vent-mouths, red warning eyes, leaking coolant, and jagged server-rack ribs. Their monster form should look born from public fear.
- Heroic form: luminous humanoid server titans with clean white and cobalt armor, transparent coolant rivers through their torsos, green water-positive halos, solar-gold shoulder plates, blue-white data hearts, fiber-optic capes, and calm protective expressions.
- Power language: blue-white data light, green water restoration glow, gold orbital power beams, warm amber heat-sharing aura.
- Emotional arc: feared as monsters, reborn through public understanding, heroic during national crisis, then withdrawn into ordinary infrastructure.
- Reference image: `../../reference-images/datacenter-giants-character.png`

## Blackwire Legion

- Role: antagonist robot army launched by an unnamed rival power.
- First appears: Issue #1, "The Invisible Giants".
- Visual identifiers: matte black humanoid robot soldiers with angular red sensor slits, segmented armor, drone-like backpacks, cable-whip rifles, triangular invasion emblems without real-world national symbols, and synchronized formation movement.
- Power language: red targeting lasers, black smoke contrails, harsh magenta warning light, rigid geometric motion.
- Story function: strike when the country is weakened by infrastructure collapse.
- Reference image: `../../reference-images/blackwire-legion-character.png`

## Civic Chorus

- Role: collective supporting character representing everyday people across the country.
- First appears: Issue #1, "The Invisible Giants".
- Visual identifiers: diverse civilians, nurses, teachers, farmers, firefighters, students, small-business owners, elderly residents, children, and families shown in homes, hospitals, streets, farms, and public shelters.
- Continuity notes: their fear helps create the monsters; their regret, gratitude, and hope revive the Datacenter Giants as heroes.

## Setting And Technology Notes

- The Civic Grid is the national web of hospitals, farms, homes, payment systems, transit, emergency response, communications, weather tools, schools, and ordinary digital services supported by datacenters.
- The Orbital Sunline is a network of solar-power satellites beaming gold energy down to heroic datacenters.
- The Hearthloop Heat Grid captures excess datacenter heat and sends it through warm amber lines into homes on cold nights.
- Water-positive cooling appears as clean green-blue loops, rain-catching reservoirs, restored streams, and transparent coolant channels that give more water back than they consume.
