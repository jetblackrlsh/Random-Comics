## Page 1: Cover

- Story role: Signal a supernatural western crisis where Super Cowboy is imprisoned in church glass while a stolen soul must still be saved.
- Layout: Single dramatic cover image with realistic photo-comic cover feel.
- Caption/title text: Cover title text: "Super Cowboy". Smaller issue title text: "Stained Glass Souls".
- Visual beats: Inside Dustfall Church House, mourners and angry townspeople look up at a tall arched stained-glass window showing Super Cowboy trapped in colored panes, his blue masked eyes faintly alive behind glass. In the window's glow, the Soul-Thief Wizard rides a twisted broom into the desert night carrying a small golden child-spirit light. Super Cowboy's electric blue-white Light Lasso glows as a trapped glass motif around him.
- Prompt notes: Realistic candid photo-comic cover, not painted poster art. Readable title text generated directly inside the cover image.

## Page 2: The Window At The Funeral

- Story role: Establish the failed rescue, the stolen girl's funeral, Super Cowboy's public shame, and the risk of destroying the window.
- Layout: Four large panels, alternating church interior and Super Cowboy's trapped view through glass.
- Caption boxes: "The wizard left Dustfall on a broom, carrying the girl's soul like a lantern." "At her funeral, her family looked up at the new window and saw the hero who had almost saved her." "Some thanked Super Cowboy for trying. Others shouted that trying had not been enough." "Behind the glass, he heard every word and had no answer."
- Visual beats: Panel 1 shows the wizard silhouetted against the desert sky outside town with a gold child-spirit glow in one hand. Panel 2 shows mourners around a closed small coffin in Dustfall Church House beneath the Super Cowboy window. Panel 3 shows mixed reactions: grieving relatives reaching up in gratitude while others point angrily at the window. Panel 4 shows Super Cowboy inside colored glass, hand against the pane, ashamed and silent.

"Outside, anger moved from the church house into Dustfall's streets." "A doomsayer called the window an idol. Super Cowboy's supporters blocked the aisle." "If they broke the glass too soon, they might break the only road home."

## Page 4: The Spirit Road

- Story role: Turn Super Cowboy from escape-seeker to rescuer by making him reason out the soul-world connection.
- Layout: Four large contemplative panels.
- Caption boxes: "The wizard had not trapped his body. He had trapped what made his body live." "If Super Cowboy's soul could stand inside a window, then the window touched the spirit world." "He stopped looking for an exit." "He started looking for the girl."
- Visual beats: Super Cowboy studies his translucent hands and the Light Lasso's reflection in stained glass; ghostly echoes of Dustfall and the church flicker around him; he kneels beside a pane where faint childlike golden light appears far below; he steps off the glass path into a darker region where jewel colors fade toward purple-black spirit fog.
