# Treatment

## Title

Stained Glass Souls

## Issue Number

3

## Logline

After a soul-thief wizard traps Super Cowboy inside a stained-glass church window and steals a little girl's spirit, Super Cowboy turns his shame into a search through the spirit world and frees every soul powering the wizard's magic.

## Central Conflict

Super Cowboy fails to stop the wizard's first crime and becomes a public symbol of that failure, trapped where the people of Dustfall can praise him, blame him, and debate whether destroying the window would save their faith or doom their hero.

## Theme

Heroism is not proven by never failing. It is proven by refusing peace, praise, or escape while someone vulnerable still needs rescue.

## Moral Message

A hero who accepts blame honestly can turn guilt into responsibility instead of despair.

## Ending

Super Cowboy rejects an angel's offer of heavenly rest, opens the wizard's spiritual prison with an overcharged Light Lasso surge, and bursts from the stained-glass window with the rescued girl as the church crowd looks up in awe.

## Emotional Arc

Super Cowboy begins humiliated and trapped, forced to listen as mourners thank him for trying and condemn him for failing. He wanders the stained-glass realm looking for an exit, then reframes the trap as a clue: if his soul was put into glass, the window may touch the spirit world. His shame becomes purpose, his combat confidence returns against the damned spirits, and his temptation to accept Heaven becomes the final test of whether he still chooses the unfinished rescue over his own reward.

## Story Shape

Eight pages: cover, funeral and trapped hero, endless stained-glass prison and town unrest, deduction and descent into the spirit world, demon fight, angel's offer and Super Cowboy's refusal, wizard's spiritual prison and Light Lasso overcharge, rescue through the shattered window.
