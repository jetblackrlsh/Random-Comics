Giant eraser shavings blew through the wind. Partially erased structures and people looked as if they were becoming sketches before fading away into a white void. Superhero Mandroid wondered if this was the end of the world. He had spent so much time fighting demon invaders from portals, never knowing what that might lead to. He had hoped that one day he'd beat the last one, and that all the portals would be closed forever.

He hoped that the source of the destruction could be that last portal. He hoped that when he found it and defeated it, the world would finally be safe for good. But when he used his rocket fists to fly over to the epicenter of the chaos, all he found was a pencil-drawn stick figure of a man standing beneath a floating circular device made of glowing sketch lines. The device looked like an impossible artist's compass, with a rotating inner dial that shifted between blue-white creation light and gray-white eraser static. The stick figure man looked deep in thought and concerned as the compass hovered over his head.

The stick figure lifted one hand, and the compass shifted into Draw Mode. Blue-white drafting lines spilled from the device like living ink, sketching a woman into reality one careful line at a time. The woman blinked, breathed, and became solid. The stick figure stared at her with uncertainty. Then the compass clicked into Erase Mode, and gray-white static washed over the woman as eraser shavings began to rain from the sky.

Superhero Mandroid leaped into action and fired a rocket fist at the floating compass. The punch did not break it. Instead, the impact knocked the rotating dial out of alignment and forced the entire device back into Draw Mode. The eraser static vanished. The rain of eraser shavings slowed. The stick figure looked up at Mandroid with a weak smile and said, "Oh hey, there you are, hero! I was hoping you'd come by and help me."

Superhero Mandroid was confused and disturbed. He demanded to know who the stick figure was and why he was destroying the world. Mandroid looked around, but he didn't see a portal anywhere. There was only the fading city, the white void, the nervous stick figure, and the hovering Reality Drafting Compass. Mandroid wondered where this strange man could have come from.

The stick figure said there were no portals today. He explained that he couldn't make any portals if he wasn't inspired. Superhero Mandroid froze in shock and asked, "So you're the one who has been behind all this?!" The stick figure nodded proudly and said, "Yes, but without any ideas, this all comes to an end." Mandroid, obviously conflicted, said, "While I'm glad there won't be any more portals, I can't stand by and let you erase my world."

The compass tried to switch back into Erase Mode. Its gray-white light pulsed, and the ground beneath Mandroid's feet began turning into loose sketch marks. Superhero Mandroid fired another rocket fist at the compass to stop it. The punch struck the rotating dial, but instead of damaging the device, the rocket fist was erased from reality. Mandroid stared in horror at the empty space where his fist should have returned.

The stick figure calmly raised his hand. The compass flared in Draw Mode, and blue-white sketch lines wrapped around Mandroid's missing arm. A new rocket fist formed from clean pencil strokes, then became solid metal. Superhero Mandroid flexed his restored hand, stunned by what had just happened. The stick figure smiled sadly and said, "See? I'm not trying to end the world. I'm trying to remember how to keep drawing it."

The stick figure explained that this entire world subsisted on his imagination. The portals, the monsters, the heroes, the city, and even Mandroid himself were all part of a living story that needed new ideas to survive. Without inspiration, the Reality Drafting Compass defaulted to erasing unfinished thoughts. The world was not being destroyed out of cruelty. It was collapsing because the imagination that held it together had gone empty.

Superhero Mandroid looked around and noticed a half-erased library nearby. The walls were fading into rough sketches, the shelves were missing corners, and books hovered between solid paper and loose pencil lines. Mandroid led the stick figure inside. Together, they sat in the half-erased library and read comics. They read stories about courage, impossible worlds, strange heroes, and battles that mattered because someone cared enough to imagine them.

The more they read, the steadier the stick figure's hand became. The Reality Drafting Compass began to glow with blue-white light. The eraser shavings stopped falling. The erased people returned as sketches first, then color, then living flesh. Buildings redrew themselves from the foundation up, and the white void pulled back like fog before sunrise.

Inspired by the stories he had read, the stick figure got to work redrawing reality. The compass spun above him, no longer twitching between creation and destruction. It drew windows, streets, homes, clouds, memories, and hope. Then a new portal opened in the sky. This time, instead of wishing the portal was the last one, Superhero Mandroid looked up and wished the portals would never end.
