# Treatment

## Title

A Smile Is Real

## Issue Number

17

## Continuity Label

Mainline. This issue follows Superhero Mandroid's revelation in Issue #15 that the Sketchmaker's imagination, comics, portals, and monsters sustain his reality.

## Logline

While battling a string of portal invasions, Superhero Mandroid grieves the peaceful future he once wanted and chooses a new purpose: protecting drawn lives because their joy is still real.

## Central Conflict

Superhero Mandroid still performs his duty with precision, but every portal now carries a contradiction. He was built to stop the invasions and protect the innocent, yet he knows that a world without portals would mean no more stories and no more world. His old dream of retiring with Dr. Dawes on a couch feels impossible. He has to decide whether endless fictional struggle makes his heroism meaningless or whether the smiles he protects matter even more because someone is turning the page to find them.

## Theme

A fictional world can still contain real courage, real kindness, and real joy for the people inside it and for the reader who needs it.

## Ending

After surviving a final grotesque portal assault, Mandroid senses the unseen reader beyond the panels. He no longer asks for the portals to end. He accepts that he may fight after Dawes is gone, after the city changes, and after his artificial body ages. His peace is not retirement. His peace is knowing every saved smile, drawn or not, reaches someone.

## Emotional Arc

Mandroid begins in active hero mode, fighting giant machines and monsters while privately mourning the loss of his old retirement fantasy. His battles become a meditation on purpose: he remembers his origin, his bond with Dr. Dawes, and the revelation that his world is drawn. At the lowest point, he imagines himself aged and rusting in an unrecognizable future with no end to the portals. Instead of despairing, he reframes endless duty as endless opportunity. He ends calm, smiling toward the reader, because helping someone smile with every flip of the page is a true heroic calling.

## Story Shape

Eight pages: cover, giant robot battle, giant scorpion rescue, drilling machine disaster, quiet retirement fantasy with Dr. Dawes, aged-future vision, alien fish-men portal fight, and a final reader-facing resolution where Mandroid understands that a drawn smile can still be real.

## New Continuity Elements

No new recurring characters, settings, or key items are introduced. The portal enemies in this issue are one-off mission threats used as a montage of Mandroid's continuing duty.
