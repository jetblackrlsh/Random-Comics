# Treatment

## Title

The Puppet Signal

## Issue Number

10

## Logline

A discarded human cybersecurity hero tries to turn Superhero Mandroid into his puppet, forcing Dr. Dawes to enter the android's mechanical mind before the hack can take hold.

## Central Conflict

Todd Phillips believes machines stole his career, his status, and the heroic recognition he thinks he earned. Superhero Mandroid is the public symbol of that theft in Todd's mind: a machine praised as a hero while a human defender is forgotten. Todd attacks from the shadows, trying to hijack Mandroid for theft and revenge. Mandroid and Dr. Dawes must learn the rules of the intrusion quickly enough to stop a human enemy who can hide outside the usual portal battlefield.

## Theme

Heroism is not proven by recognition or job title; it is proven by what someone chooses to protect when their pride is wounded.

## Ending

Mandroid and Dr. Dawes delete the virus swarm with digital hardlight weapons and trace the attack to Todd Phillips. The authorities reach Todd's apartment after he has already fled. Todd remains free, poorer and angrier than before, swearing that Superhero Mandroid will one day belong to him.

## Emotional Arc

Todd begins as a man who believes his invisible service should have made him beloved, then twists that grief into hatred of machines. Mandroid begins the issue confident in his lab's portal watch, becomes helpless inside his own mind, and regains agency by remembering that his holo shield can defeat digital beings. Dr. Dawes moves from caretaker panic to direct risk, proving that his bond with Mandroid is more than invention and maintenance.

## Story Shape

Eight pages: cover, Todd's fall, Todd's hack and Mandroid's collapse, Dr. Dawes activating the Lyoko-Tron-Matrix protocol, Mandroid overwhelmed in the cyberspace grid, Dr. Dawes explaining the digital weapon rule, shield-and-sword deletion of the virus swarm, and Todd escaping into recurring villain status.
