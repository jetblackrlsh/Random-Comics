# Treatment

## Title

Guardian Demon

## Issue Number

16

## Continuity Label

Mainline. Paul Teethings, the possessed exorcist introduced in Issue #13, finds kinship with Mandroid City's AI-augmented transhuman community and becomes their frightening protector after a cross-marked paramilitary cell attacks their meet-up.

## Logline

When Paul Teethings attends a transhuman meet-up hoping someone will understand what it feels like to be beyond human, a violent religious paramilitary attack forces him to turn his demonic curse into the shield his new community needs.

## Central Conflict

Paul hates the demon inside him, but flight, claws, strength, and fire have awakened a terrifying freedom he cannot deny. The transhuman community's AI companions, neural wearables, exoskeletons, and body augmentations make him wonder whether his monstrous change can still belong among people who choose to go beyond human limits. Acceptance gives him hope, but the same religious power structure that rejected him sends cross-marked paramilitary attackers against the transhumans. Paul must decide whether powering up the demon means surrendering to it or taking control of it for someone else's protection.

## Theme

Being feared as a monster does not decide whether someone becomes one. Protection is a choice.

## Ending

Paul drives the paramilitary attackers away without letting the demon take ownership of his will. The transhumans do not see a perfect saint or a safe symbol. They see someone terrifying who stood between them and the people trying to hurt them. Superhero Mandroid remains their image of future acceptance, but Paul becomes their present-tense defender: Protector Paul, the guardian demon.

## Emotional Arc

Paul begins ashamed of enjoying his demonic powers, especially flight. He becomes nervous that even transhumans will recoil from his wings and ember-lit throat. Their leader's understanding reframes his curse as a kind of unwanted transcendence, and her comparison to Superhero Mandroid's Version One gives him hope that society can learn to see past frightening forms. When the attack comes, Paul's hope turns into anger, but he channels that anger into defense rather than revenge. He ends the issue still demonic, still conflicted, but no longer alone.

## Story Shape

Eight pages: cover, Paul discovering the joy and shame of flight, Paul entering the transhuman meet-up, the transhuman leader connecting Paul's form to society's fear of Mandroid Version One, the cross-marked paramilitary attack, Paul powering up to defend the community, Paul using shadow speed, fire breath, wings, and claws to disarm the attackers, and the transhumans naming the meaning of their new guardian demon.
