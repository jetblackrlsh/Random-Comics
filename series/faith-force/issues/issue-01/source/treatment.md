# Treatment

## Title

The Portal That Would Not Close

## Issue Number

1

## Logline

Earth's greatest martial artists interrupt a cult's Hell portal ritual when Marcus Hale's faith in his team ignites the golden aura of Faith Force.

## Central Conflict

Marcus Hale, Serena Park, and Darius Cross must stop the Hellgate cult before their ritual opens a portal for the Demon King. At first the conflict is physical: elite martial artists against warrior monks trained to guard the ceremony. The deeper conflict begins when the monks surrender their humanity to demon familiars and prove that ordinary technique cannot overcome dark magic alone.

## Theme

Mortal skill becomes something greater when it is joined to trust. Faith Force is born because Marcus believes in his friends as fiercely as any saint believes in heaven.

## Ending

The team defeats the imps and scatters the cultists, but the portal has already begun to open. The ritual is delayed instead of erased. The final image leaves the heroes watching the unstable red-black wound in reality, knowing they must train for the Demon King's return.

## Emotional Arc

The heroes begin confident, even outnumbered, because martial discipline has made them the best human fighters alive. The imp transformation breaks that confidence and forces them to admit that the coming war cannot be won by technique alone. Marcus turns fear into faith, Serena and Darius answer that faith, and the team ends the issue changed: still mortal, but no longer limited to mortal force.

## Story Shape

Eight pages: cover, the heroes approach the Hellgate Foundry, human martial arts against warrior monks, the monks transform into imps, dark magic overwhelms the team, Marcus ignites Faith Force, the golden aura defeats the imps, and the team delays the portal but learns the Demon King is still coming.

Use clear rectangular caption boxes generated inside the page art. Captions should explain stakes, powers, and emotional turns directly enough for a reader to follow the supernatural escalation without later text overlays.
