# Page Script

All pages are full modern anime comic pages in 4:5 portrait aspect ratio. Use highly saturated color, bright glow, sharp detail, dynamic shots and posing, crisp panel borders, and clean readable caption boxes. Caption-box text must be generated directly inside the page art. Use caption boxes only, no speech bubbles, no thought bubbles, no floating subtitles, no blank text boxes, no watermarks, and no later text overlay.

## Page 1: Cover

- Story role: Present Amnie Zia as a forgotten god whose dinosaur power, lost memories, heroic instinct, and bee-themed nemesis all collide.
- Layout: Single dramatic anime cover image.
- Caption/title text: Cover title text: "Dream Anime Chronicles". Issue title text: "The God Who Forgot Tomorrow".
- Visual beats: Amnie Zia stands in a luminous heroic pose at the center of Mnemosyne Junction, long flowing silver hair like strands of light, pale iridescent skin, deep cosmic-blue star-specked eyes, cosmic-blue starry unitard with silver trim, silver gloves, silver boots, and the silver T-Rex head insignia bright on their chest. Behind them, a T-Rex silhouette made of star-blue reality energy roars through cracked memory arches while amber bee drones and honeycomb barriers swarm the skyline.

## Page 2: The God With Missing Names

- Story role: Introduce Amnie's appearance, lost divinity, amnesia, heroic instinct, and dinosaur-limited power.
- Layout: Four panels: memory fracture, Amnie close-up, dinosaur summon, saved civilian.
- Caption boxes: "Amnie Zia woke with the shape of a god and almost none of the memories." "Their eyes still held stars, but their past answered in broken light." "Reality should have obeyed them." "So far, it mostly sent dinosaurs."
- Visual beats: Amnie drifts above ancient ruins inside a modern city, silver hair flowing like light; close-up of their cosmic-blue starry eyes and worried androgynous face; Amnie raises a silver-gloved hand and accidentally summons a small glowing velociraptor instead of a clean reality bridge; they instinctively use it to pull a civilian away from falling rubble.

## Page 3: The Hive In The Crossing

- Story role: Inciting crisis: a bee-themed supervillain traps Mnemosyne Junction and mocks Amnie's incomplete godhood.
- Layout: Three large panels with escalating danger.
- Caption boxes: "The bee-themed villain always found the wound in Amnie's doubt." "Mnemosyne Junction became a hive of amber walls and panicked streets." "A god without memory, the villain laughed, is just a miracle with poor aim."
- Visual beats: Black-and-gold bee-armored villain silhouette appears on giant amber honeycomb projections; mechanical bee drones seal streets with hexagon barriers; civilians trapped between glass towers and marble memory arches; Amnie lands in a battle stance, silver T-Rex insignia glowing faintly.

## Page 4: Too Much Thunder

- Story role: First failed tactic: Amnie tries spectacular dinosaur force and makes the crisis worse.
- Layout: Four action panels moving from confidence to embarrassment.
- Caption boxes: "Amnie answered mockery with size." "A thunder-lizard tore through the hive wall." "The villain bent the swarm around every heroic roar." "The street was safer before Amnie tried to prove what they were."
- Visual beats: Amnie summons a gigantic luminous T-Rex with star-blue claws; the T-Rex smashes an amber wall but falling debris threatens civilians; bee drones redirect the dinosaur with honeycomb light; final panel shows Amnie horrified as people flee from both the swarm and the dinosaur.

## Page 5: The Small Things They Could Not Fix

- Story role: Public criticism deepens Amnie's shame and shows the daily-problem burden from the wiki lore.
- Layout: Four panels: rescue, complaint, mundane needs, Amnie isolated.
- Caption boxes: "Amnie saved lives." "Then the saved asked why a god never fixed the rest." "Broken homes. Empty clinics. Streets that flooded every season." "Every ordinary need became another name Amnie could not remember."
- Visual beats: Amnie carries civilians away from a damaged bus while bee drones regroup overhead; rescued citizens point at cracked apartment windows, sick family members, and flooded side streets; Amnie's silver hair dims as their cosmic-blue eyes fill with guilt; the bee villain's amber silhouette watches from a billboard.

## Page 6: The Door Behind The Stars

- Story role: Temptation and second failure: a memory fragment opens and nearly pulls Amnie away from the crisis.
- Layout: Five panels with surreal divine imagery.
- Caption boxes: "Then the hive struck something older than pain." "A door opened in Amnie's missing past." "They saw a throne among forgotten gods." "They saw power enough to rewrite the whole disaster." "And below it, one child screamed."
- Visual beats: Amber bee drones crack a marble memory arch; starry silver-blue portal opens behind Amnie showing silhouettes of gods and a radiant empty throne; Amnie reaches toward the memory, tears glowing in cosmic-blue eyes; below, a child is trapped under a collapsing honeycomb barrier; final panel shows Amnie frozen between memory and rescue.

## Page 7: One Life First

- Story role: Defining choice: Amnie chooses immediate protection over chasing full memory.
- Layout: Four panels, quieter and focused.
- Caption boxes: "Amnie let the throne fade." "A perfect god might have saved everything." "Amnie was not perfect." "Amnie was here."
- Visual beats: Amnie turns away from the divine memory portal; close-up of the silver T-Rex insignia shining hard on their chest; Amnie kneels beside the trapped child with silver-gloved hands steady; a small controlled pack of glowing dinosaurs forms around them, not monstrous but protective and precise.

## Page 8: The Shape Of A Beginning

- Story role: Resolution: Amnie wins by coordinating focused dinosaur rescue instead of overwhelming spectacle.
- Layout: Four panels ending with a triumphant rescue splash.
- Caption boxes: "The dinosaurs did not need to prove a god." "They lifted what was falling." "They carried who could not run." "They made room for Amnie to save the day they could actually reach."
- Visual beats: A triceratops braces a collapsing amber wall; pterosaurs carry civilians over blocked streets; small raptors herd people away from drone paths; final splash shows Amnie standing with the rescued child as the T-Rex energy insignia flares, the bee villain's projection cracks, and Mnemosyne Junction opens again.

## Page 9: Amnie Zia Character Poster

- Story role: Final character poster that summarizes the complete issue and reinforces Amnie's defining traits.
- Layout: One full-page poster composition with Amnie in a strong full-body pose beside one large readable caption box.
- Caption box text: "Amnie Zia is an amnesiac god with long silver hair, iridescent pale skin, cosmic-blue starry eyes, and a blue star-specked unitard marked by a silver T-Rex. Their full reality-shaping power is hidden behind missing memories, leaving dinosaur command as the ability they can trust. A bee-themed villain mocks their limits, and the people they save ask why a god cannot fix every ordinary pain. Amnie's first victory is choosing one life first. Protect now. Remember later."
- Visual beats: Amnie stands in a heroic anime poster pose in Mnemosyne Junction, silver hair flowing, cosmic-blue unitard and silver boots clear, T-Rex insignia shining. Star-blue dinosaur spirits circle protectively behind them while cracked amber honeycomb pieces fall away. The memory throne glows faintly in the sky but no longer pulls Amnie from the people below. The large caption box occupies a clean readable side area without covering Amnie's silhouette.
