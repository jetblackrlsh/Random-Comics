# Page Script

All pages are full modern anime comic pages in 4:5 portrait aspect ratio. Use highly saturated color, bright glow, sharp detail, dynamic shots and posing, crisp panel borders, and clean caption boxes. Caption-box text must be generated directly inside the page art. Use caption boxes only, no speech bubbles, no thought bubbles, no floating subtitles, no blank text boxes, no watermarks, and no later text overlay.

## Page 1: Cover

- Story role: Present Arma Geddon as a cosmic wanderer whose destructive energy and desire for peace are in direct conflict.
- Layout: Single dramatic anime cover image.
- Caption/title text: Cover title text: "Dream Anime Chronicles". Issue title text: "The World That Still Breathed".
- Visual beats: Arma Geddon floats in space above a cracked planet split by glowing purple-white fissures. Their faceless glowing purple head tilts toward the broken world, long glowing purple dreadlocks drifting in zero gravity, orange T-shirt and black athletic pants bright against the stars, white lightning aura roaring around them. Translucent demon silhouettes spiral through the aura like pieces of a storm.

## Page 2: The Wanderer Who Feared Arrival

- Story role: Explain Arma's identity, powers, fear, and lonely cosmic life.
- Layout: Four panels: space flight, faceless close-up, energy blast memory, lonely drifting silhouette.
- Caption boxes: "Arma Geddon could cross space without a ship." "Their body needed no air, no food, and no shelter from the stars." "But every heartbeat carried life energy strong enough to erase a landscape." "So Arma wandered alone, afraid that peace would shatter if anyone came too close."
- Visual beats: Arma flies through a bright star field wrapped in white lightning; close-up of smooth faceless purple head and glowing dreadlocks; memory panel of a white energy blast tearing across a planet surface; Arma curls in empty space with demon silhouettes barely visible in the aura.

## Page 3: The Planet That Remembered

- Story role: Bring Arma back to the home world they destroyed and reveal new life in the ruins.
- Layout: Three large panels with scale and emotion.
- Caption boxes: "The world Arma broke still waited." "Its ancient cities lay half-buried in the wound." "And inside the cracks, impossible gardens had begun to breathe."
- Visual beats: Wide shot of the shattered planet with deep luminous fissures; Arma descends through thin atmosphere over ruined advanced towers and floating debris; close view of strange glowing plant life growing from the energy-rich cracks while Arma hovers above it in stunned silence.

## Page 4: The Failure Of Distance

- Story role: First failed tactic: Arma tries to protect life by withdrawing, but the planet's stored energy surges without guidance.
- Layout: Four panels, moving from retreat to danger.
- Caption boxes: "Arma's first answer was distance." "If their power caused the wound, they would not touch the healing." "But the planet had learned Arma's energy too well." "Without control, the old storm began to wake."
- Visual beats: Arma backs away from the glowing plants, arms tight against their body; the fissures pulse brighter as if recognizing Arma; white-purple lightning erupts from the cracks toward the gardens; Arma turns in panic as tiny glowing leaves begin to burn.

## Page 5: The Failure Of Force

- Story role: Second failed tactic: Arma uses a direct energy blast, making the crisis worse.
- Layout: Five action panels.
- Caption boxes: "So Arma chose force." "They aimed one clean blast at the surge." "Fear made the blast too bright." "The planet answered with every buried echo." "Arma had not stopped the disaster. They had reminded it how to begin."
- Visual beats: Arma braces above the fissure with aura exploding outward; a white lightning beam strikes the rising storm; the beam fractures into wild branches; ancient ruins crack apart and hover; final panel shows the glowing gardens threatened by a circular shockwave while Arma recoils.

## Page 6: The Failure Of Command

- Story role: Third failed tactic: Arma summons demons as separate tools, but commanding them through fear turns them unstable.
- Layout: Five panels, supernatural and strategic.
- Caption boxes: "Arma called the demons from their own life energy." "They ordered the shapes to hold the storm down." "The demons obeyed the command." "They also obeyed the fear beneath it." "A frightened army only multiplied the panic."
- Visual beats: Translucent white-purple demon silhouettes emerge from Arma's lightning aura; Arma points sharply toward the fissures like a commander; the demons pin arcs of energy to the ground; their silhouettes distort into chaotic snarls; Arma is surrounded by unstable demon lightning while the gardens flicker.

## Page 7: The Parts Arma Tried To Bury

- Story role: Low point and realization: Arma recognizes the demons as extensions of self, not outsiders to dominate.
- Layout: Four panels, emotional focus.
- Caption boxes: "At the center of the storm, Arma saw the truth." "The demons were not invaders." "They were fear given shape." "And fear could not be mastered by pretending it belonged to someone else."
- Visual beats: Arma kneels on cracked ground between burning glowing plants and the abyss of the fissure; demon silhouettes reflect Arma's faceless head and purple dreadlocks; memory image of the home planet splitting under a panic blast; Arma reaches gently toward one demon instead of commanding it.

## Page 8: The Power That Chose Protection

- Story role: Resolve the crisis by showing Arma's defining strategy: acceptance, precision, and protective life energy.
- Layout: Four panels ending with a luminous rescue splash.
- Caption boxes: "Arma stopped running from the storm inside them." "They let every demon orbit close." "The blast became a breath." "For the first time, Arma's power chose what it would save."
- Visual beats: Arma rises calmly, demons circling as disciplined white-purple constellations; Arma's white lightning aura narrows into fine threads instead of explosions; the threads weave around the glowing gardens and cracked ruins; final splash panel shows a protective lattice spanning the fissures while new plants shine safely beneath it.

## Page 9: Arma Geddon Character Poster

- Story role: Final character poster that summarizes the complete issue and reinforces Arma's defining traits.
- Layout: One full-page poster composition with Arma in a dynamic full-body pose beside one large readable caption box.
- Caption box text: "Arma Geddon is a faceless purple cosmic wanderer with glowing dreadlocks, an orange shirt, black athletic pants, white sneakers, and a storm of white lightning life energy. They can fly through space, survive without ordinary needs, fire devastating energy blasts, and summon invisible demons from their own consciousness. Arma destroyed their home planet in a panic and exiled themself to protect the universe. When new life on the shattered world is threatened, Arma fails by fleeing, blasting, and commanding fear. Their true power appears when they accept the demons as part of themself and turn destructive energy into protection."
- Visual beats: Arma stands in a strong modern anime poster pose on a luminous fissure bridge, faceless purple head lifted, glowing purple dreadlocks streaming upward, orange T-shirt, black athletic pants, white socks and sneakers, white lightning aura forming a halo. Disciplined translucent demons orbit behind them, and the cracked planet's glowing gardens shine below. The large caption box occupies a clean readable side area without covering the silhouette.
