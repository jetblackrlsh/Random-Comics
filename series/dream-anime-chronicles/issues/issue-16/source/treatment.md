# Treatment

## Title

The Show That Needed A Battery

## Issue Number

16

## Logline

Bato Leo, a lazy living battery at an interdimensional hotel, tries to coast through a supernatural animal show, but only earns respect after facing the animals they fear and powering the hotel with careful coordination instead of panic.

## Central Conflict

Bato wants an easy shift, a quiet nap, and enough respect from hotel staff and guests that nobody treats them like a walking power outlet. The hotel assigns Bato to coordinate the Celestial Creature Showcase, the exact event Bato hates because supernatural animals dislike their electric aura. When a portal surge cuts power to the hotel and panics every creature in the show ring, Bato must choose between hiding from the animals or becoming the steady battery and real coordinator everyone needs.

## Theme

Respect is not won by avoiding responsibility. It is earned when power is used with care for people and creatures who are afraid too.

## Ending

Bato stops blasting electricity like a defense reflex and listens to the scared animals' reactions. They convert their stored charge into a gentle rhythmic backup grid, light safe portal paths one by one, and guide the creatures, guests, and hotel staff through the crisis. The animals still do not love Bato, but they stop panicking around Bato's controlled current. The hotel manager lets Bato keep a promised break because Bato actually did the work first.

## Emotional Arc

Bato begins relaxed, evasive, and annoyed that everyone expects the living battery to be useful on command. Their fear of animals makes the event feel unfair, so they try to solve the job from a distance and do as little as possible. Each shortcut fails because the creatures can sense careless electricity and the guests can see Bato avoiding the real problem. At the low point, Bato realizes the animals are not simply rejecting them; they are scared of uncontrolled power. Bato finishes as the same easygoing person, but with a clearer sense that respect comes from controlled effort, not from pretending nothing matters.

## Story Shape

Nine pages: cover, hotel setup, Bato's avoided assignment, the creature show begins badly, the portal-power crisis, Bato's failed shortcut, the low point where Bato understands the animals' fear, Bato's coordinated rescue, and a final Bato Leo poster-summary page.
