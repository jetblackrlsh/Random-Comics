Issue Four: Gravity Redemption

Story Page 1
The instant the Reality Breakers arrive in Shadowgarde City, a massive slab of broken pavement rips free from the street and flies straight toward them. Jack reacts first, summoning a giant cosmic slingshot made of purple energy between the team and the incoming projectile. The construct catches the hurtling debris, stretches under the force, and then snaps forward with explosive power. The rogue gravity student barely dodges as the chunk of pavement rockets back past him and shatters against a nearby tower. Around them, the battlefield becomes clear: civilians are trapped, gravity itself is unstable, and this first Field Trip is already a life-or-death mission.

Story Page 2
Jack immediately takes command, telling Miss Aurora that the civilians are the top priority and ordering Blaze to protect her while she keeps her shields active. Aurora throws up radiant barriers of sunlight around the endangered bystanders, sealing them inside glowing domes just as warped debris begins to rain down from above. Blaze plants himself beside her with fire dancing around his fists, ready to intercept anything that breaks through. With the civilians temporarily secured, Jack creates cosmic wing constructs and launches himself upward toward the rogue student. Hovering in the distorted air, Jack confronts him directly while the city twists unnaturally beneath them.

Story Page 3
Instead of attacking immediately, Jack studies the rogue and asks a question that cuts deeper than any blow: why has Shadowgarde Academy allowed this destruction to continue in its own city without sending its own students to stop him? The rogue sneers at first, but the question clearly unsettles him. Jack presses harder, pointing out that it makes no sense for another academy to respond first unless something about this situation is not what the rogue believes. Below them, Blaze mutters that Jack had better know what he is doing, while Aurora struggles to hold her shields steady against the shifting pressure. The rogue's gravity field flickers for a moment, and Jack realizes that doubt is creating the first real opening.

Story Page 4
Jack continues, suggesting that if Shadowgarde truly trusted the rogue, then the school would already have reached out or intervened instead of leaving him isolated. The rogue lashes out defensively, insisting that his academy trusts him and that he is not unstable, but his voice cracks under the strain. Miss Aurora backs Jack up from below, shouting that perhaps Shadowgarde is testing him to see whether he will actually endanger innocent people. The words hit hard, and the rogue loses control for a second as a violent gravity blast slams into Aurora's shield. The barrier cracks under the impact, the civilians scream, and the mission suddenly teeters on the edge of disaster.

Story Page 5
Seeing the danger escalate, Jack shifts from suspicion to understanding. He tells the rogue that academies like Power Academy and Shadowgarde do not judge students on raw power alone, because hero ranking depends on simulation performance, control, and moral judgment. Jack points out that gravity manipulation is obviously powerful, which means the rogue's real problem is not lack of potential but the way he is handling his frustration. The rogue hesitates, torn between anger and the desperate need to be recognized. Blaze keeps watch over Aurora and the civilians, ready to strike if Jack's gamble fails, while the warped air trembles with unstable force. For the first time, the rogue stops talking like a conqueror and starts sounding like a student who feels abandoned.

Story Page 6
Jack pushes the point with calm precision, telling the rogue that respect and recognition will only come if he proves he has discipline, self-control, and the morals expected of a hero. He says that if the rogue really wants a higher rank, then this rampage is destroying the very future he claims to want. The words finally break through, and the rogue admits that Shadowgarde called him unstable and unpredictable, accusations that clearly wounded his pride more than he wanted to admit. Aurora, still bracing her damaged shields, tells him that surrendering and choosing to do better would be a real act of strength. With a shaky exhale, the rogue's gravity distortions begin to settle, and the floating debris slowly lowers back to the ground.

Story Page 7
The rogue descends at last, shoulders slumped and hostility draining out of him as he reluctantly agrees to stand down. Moments later, the Shadowgarde enforcement squad arrives far too late to claim any real credit for saving the city. The civilians are safe, the rogue has been neutralized without being destroyed, and the Reality Breakers have unknowingly passed the moral test at the heart of their first true mission. Aurora jokes that the whole climax felt less like a battle and more like a redemption arc colliding with bureaucracy, while Blaze grumbles that he still would have preferred a simpler fight followed by food. As the team looks over the battered city and the mission's strange victory, one thing is certain: the Reality Breakers are no longer just students in a simulation.
