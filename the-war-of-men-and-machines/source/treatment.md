# Treatment

## Title

The War of Men & Machines

## Format

Standalone Random Comics one-shot, eight pages total: one cover and seven story pages.

## Publication Date

2026-08-15

## Source

Adapted from Nicholas Alexander Benson / Jet Black's 90-second short film `The War of Men & Machines`.

## Logline

When a galaxy-wide war pressures Jax Blade to betray Kindle, the mechanical hare who became his only family, their furious duel ends in a choice to stand together—just in time for a meteor catastrophe that demands everything they have.

## Central Conflict

Jax has accepted humanity's demand that biology should determine his loyalty, even though humans never loved or supported him as Kindle did. Kindle fights him because his choice feels like a betrayal of their shared life and of every machine threatened by the war. Their personal conflict can end only when Jax admits that true family is chosen through love, not inherited through species.

## Theme

War turns identities into cages. Solidarity becomes possible when people refuse those cages and choose the relationships that have actually sustained them. Machines and humans become stronger together, but reconciliation delayed by war may still carry an unbearable cost.

## Ending

Jax and Kindle combine their orange and green plasma blades to destroy a meteor swarm threatening Orbital Station Aegis. Kindle channels more power than her reactor can survive. She collapses, and the synthetic heartbeat-like vibration that once comforted Jax through lonely nights fades in his arms. They save the station and prove cooperation possible, but Jax is left holding the only family he ever truly had.

## Emotional Arc

Kindle begins furious and wounded; Jax begins defensive, ashamed, and trapped by species loyalty. Their duel strips away his rationalizations. Kindle's reminder of their shared history breaks him, and her compassion allows him to choose her openly. Their renewed trust becomes heroic unity during the meteor assault. Triumph turns immediately into terror when Kindle collapses, ending with Jax's grief and the tragic consequence of choosing love too late.

## Cast

- Kindle: sentient feminine humanoid mechanical hare, Jax's closest companion and true family, emotionally expressive despite her armored synthetic body.
- Jax Blade: human wanderer and plasma-sword fighter who betrays Kindle under pressure to side with humanity, then rejects that imposed loyalty.
- The human-machine war: never personified as a single villain; its tribal logic is the force that fractures their relationship.
- The meteor bombardment: an impersonal external catastrophe that tests whether their reconciliation can become meaningful action.

## Art Direction

Full 4:5 portrait anime comic pages with vivid orange-and-green split lighting, crisp comic linework, cinematic semi-realistic detail, expressive faces, and an operatic orbital-station setting suspended before a luminous spiral galaxy. Kindle's white-and-orange armor and amber eyes must remain consistent. Jax's black robes, blond hair, green blade, facial scars, and emotional exhaustion must remain consistent. Use caption boxes only; no speech bubbles, thought bubbles, page numbers, panel labels, logos, watermarks, or later-added story text.
