About Random Comics

Random Comics is a home for standalone one-shots, strange experiments, and comic series that grow beyond a single issue.

Some comics are based on dreams. Some come from sudden shower thoughts, weird news, superhero concepts, game-inspired ideas, or random sparks of imagination that demanded to become something real. Some ideas only need one finished comic. Others may build into recurring characters, settings, and issue-to-issue continuity.

The comics collected here may vary in tone, style, length, format, and genre. You might find superhero action, sci-fi weirdness, dream logic, horror, comedy, mythic strangeness, standalone stories, or the beginning of a larger series.

Think of this as a creative sketchbook turned into a comic archive: a place where spontaneous ideas can become finished stories, whether they stand alone or keep expanding.

Not every comic needs a universe.

But when a strange little story grows into one, Random Comics has room for that too.

Superhero Mandroid

An android superhero built to repel interdimensional threats discovers that the enemies beyond the portals have learned how to hunt him at home.

Characters

Character Descriptions Character: Superhero Mandroid - First appears: Issue 1, "The Demon at the Door" - Role: protagonist, android superhero designed to protect the world from interdimensional threats. - Visual identifiers: human-looking adult man with white synthetic skin, slicked-back brown hair, clean-shaven face, alert brown eyes, athletic build, red superhero unitard, yellow cape, yellow SHM chest insignia, and oversized mechanical green fists with panel seams, knuckle plating, and small thruster vents. His expression shifts from controlled suspicion to focused tactical anger. - Continuity notes: Can summon a rechargeable cyan-green hardlight holo shield and fire regenerating rapid rocket punches. He is used to fighting at portal sites, not being found in his hidden lab. - Reference image: ../reference-images/superhero-mandroid-character.png Character: The Inventor - First appears: Issue 1, "The Demon at the Door" - Role: supporting character, creator of Superhero Mandroid. - Visual identifiers: older human man in his early 60s with light skin, gray swept-back hair, tired blue eyes, narrow face, trimmed gray beard, wire-frame glasses, and a practical dark lab coat over a blue shirt. He wears a key card on a lanyard when working near the lab. - Continuity notes: He is away at a conference during Issue 1, but his identity is used by the Pixel Demon as a holographic disguise. He is one of the few people who knows the hidden lab exists. - Reference image: ../reference-images/inventor-character.png Character: Pixel Demon - First appears: Issue 1, "The Demon at the Door" - Role: antagonist, digital demon and technology infiltrator. - Visual identifiers: humanoid demon made of glowing blue pixels, fractured hardlight plates, flickering square particles, jagged horn-like pixel clusters, blank cyan eyes, clawed hands, and hologram tendrils that emerge from its back. Its body can break apart into an intangible cloud of blue pixels before reforming. - Continuity notes: Can mimic a person with a hologram disguise, form hardlight tendrils, interface with low-level technology, and become temporarily intangible by dispersing into pixels. Its hardlight body can still be cut by Superhero Mandroid's holo shield. - Reference image: ../reference-images/pixel-demon-character.png Character: Shutdown Sludge Creature - First appears: Issue 2, "Monsters Beneath Our Streets" - Role: antagonist, interdimensional portal creature. - Visual identifiers: massive purple sludge monster with a glossy tar-like body, rippling gelatinous folds, wet violet-black highlights, bulbous pseudopods, faint alien eyes or glowing cores buried under the surface, and a trail of dead electronics and darkened screens wherever it flows. It leaks downward from portals and spreads across streets like a living toxic flood. - Continuity notes: Any electronic device the creature touches immediately shuts down. Superhero Mandroid cannot safely punch or touch it directly because his android systems could also shut down. It feeds on or drains projected holo-shield energy. - Reference image: ../reference-images/shutdown-sludge-creature-character.png Character: Sewer Fatbergs - First appears: Issue 2, "Monsters Beneath Our Streets" - Role: environmental monster colony and unexpected city defenders. - Visual identifiers: sewer-dwelling masses of pale yellow-gray grease, black grime, wet trash, writhing organic texture, disease-slick surfaces, small pale larvae-like movement, and lumpy mound shapes clinging to tunnel walls and wastewater channels. They should look disgusting and dangerous without gore. - Continuity notes: Normally hidden beneath the city and handled by sewer workers, the fatbergs are diseased sludge-like urban monsters. In Issue 2, their contamination breaks down the alien purple sludge creature on contact. - Reference image: ../reference-images/sewer-fatbergs-character.png Character: Opera Singer - First appears: Issue 3, "The Voice That Broke The Hall" - Role: civilian victim and temporary antagonist while controlled by the brain parasite. - Visual identifiers: adult woman opera singer in her late 30s with medium brown skin, elegant cheekbones, dark expressive eyes, black hair pinned in a formal stage updo, and a dramatic deep emerald opera gown with gold trim and structured shoulders. On stage she carries herself with trained posture and powerful breath control. - Continuity notes: The brain parasite amplifies her already powerful voice to supernatural force. After the parasite is removed, she returns to normal and is visibly relieved but shaken. - Reference image: ../reference-images/opera-singer-character.png Character: Brain Parasite - First appears: Issue 3, "The Voice That Broke The Hall" - Role: antagonist, demon parasite and mind-control threat. - Visual identifiers: alien demon parasite shaped like a living helmet, glossy dark crimson and black chitin, ribbed organic plates, small grasping tendrils, faint violet nerve lights, and a crown-like shell that clamps over a victim's head without covering the mouth. It looks parasitic and supernatural but non-gory. - Continuity notes: Can control a host's mind and amplify the host's voice into destructive sonic blasts. It is vulnerable to being knocked loose by a precise rocket punch. - Reference image: ../reference-images/brain-parasite-character.png

Settings

Setting Descriptions Setting: Superhero Mandroid's Hidden Lab - First appears: Issue 1, "The Demon at the Door" - Visual identifiers: concealed high-tech underground laboratory with reinforced steel walls, matte black equipment racks, portal-monitoring screens, green diagnostic lights, a heavy security door with peephole and buzzer panel, tool benches, charging equipment, a reinforced retractable launch skylight above a vertical launch shaft, and subtle damage from past missions. Lighting is practical and cool, with emergency red accents after attacks. - Continuity notes: The lab is secret and hardened against low-level hacking. The Pixel Demon can discover its location through technology traces but cannot hack the door or Superhero Mandroid directly. - Reference image: ../reference-images/hidden-lab-setting.png Setting: Mandroid's City - First appears: Issue 2, "Monsters Beneath Our Streets" - Visual identifiers: dense modern city with glass towers, older brick roofs, rooftop HVAC units, street grids, emergency vehicles, crowd-filled sidewalks, power lines, and a dramatic skyline that can be viewed from above. Portal light often reflects in windows and wet pavement. - Continuity notes: The city is one of Superhero Mandroid's primary protection zones. Portal threats can open above it, forcing him to consider civilian crowds, infrastructure, and collateral damage. - Reference image: ../reference-images/mandroids-city-setting.png Setting: City Power Plant - First appears: Issue 2, "Monsters Beneath Our Streets" - Visual identifiers: industrial urban power plant with concrete walls, transformer yards, steel lattice towers, warning lights, bundled power lines, cooling stacks, fenced perimeter, and electrical infrastructure close enough to city streets that a monster could reach it. - Continuity notes: If the Shutdown Sludge Creature reaches the plant, citywide power failure becomes likely. Superhero Mandroid creates a giant holo-shield dam to protect it. - Reference image: ../reference-images/city-power-plant-setting.png Setting: City Sewers - First appears: Issue 2, "Monsters Beneath Our Streets" - Visual identifiers: large underground sewer tunnels with arched concrete walls, flowing dark wastewater channels, maintenance walkways, rusted ladders, inspection lights, dripping pipes, and dense fatberg growths along the walls and waterline. - Continuity notes: The sewer system hides fatberg colonies beneath the city. In Issue 2, Superhero Mandroid redirects the alien sludge into the sewers, where the fatbergs decay it away. - Reference image: ../reference-images/city-sewers-setting.png Setting: Grand Opera House - First appears: Issue 3, "The Voice That Broke The Hall" - Visual identifiers: ornate old opera house with red velvet seats, curved balconies, gold trim, frescoed ceiling, large chandelier, polished wooden stage, heavy burgundy curtains, orchestra pit, backstage brick and plaster walls, and enough structural depth that a rocket punch can break through the back wall into the theater. - Continuity notes: A portal opens inside the opera house during a performance, unleashing the brain parasite into a crowded civilian setting. Issue 3 leaves the theater damaged by sonic blasts, shattered seats, and a crater in the seating area. - Reference image: ../reference-images/grand-opera-house-setting.png

Key Items

Key Item Descriptions Key Item: Rechargeable Holo Shield - First appears: Issue 1, "The Demon at the Door" - Visual identifiers: translucent cyan-green hardlight shield, usually forearm-sized to full torso-sized, curved with a faint hex-grid surface, bright rim, and pulsing battery-like segments that dim when overused. It looks like solid light projected from Superhero Mandroid's wrist hardware. - Continuity notes: The shield is made of hardlight and can block, slice, or sever other hardlight constructs. It needs recharge time if drained, but Issue 1 shows it has enough charge to cut the Pixel Demon's tendril and body. - Reference image: ../reference-images/rechargeable-holo-shield-key-item.png Key Item: Regenerating Rocket Fists - First appears: Issue 1, "The Demon at the Door" - Visual identifiers: oversized green mechanical fists with heavy knuckle plating, dark seams, compact thruster vents at the wrists, internal pistons, and green-white rocket exhaust when launched. The fists look physically heavy but fast once fired. - Continuity notes: Superhero Mandroid can fire rapid projectile rocket punches that regenerate or reconnect after launch. They are his signature blunt-force attack, but they are less useful against enemies that can become intangible. - Reference image: ../reference-images/regenerating-rocket-fists-key-item.png

Issue Summaries

Issue Summaries Issue 1: The Demon at the Door - Folder: series/superhero-mandroid/issues/issue-01/ - Logline: Superhero Mandroid lets a disguised visitor into his hidden lab and has to defeat a digital demon by turning its own hardlight body against it. - Plot summary: After returning from a portal mission, Superhero Mandroid hears his secret lab buzzer and sees his inventor through the peephole. The visitor's refusal to use a key card makes him suspicious, but he opens the door and is ambushed by a Pixel Demon wearing the inventor's image as a hologram. The demon reveals it found the lab by interfacing with low-level technology and plans to kill the inventor later. Superhero Mandroid realizes the demon cannot hack the lab or his android body, uses his rechargeable holo shield to block and sever its hardlight tendrils, then slices the demon apart before it can reveal who sent it. - Continuity changes: Portal enemies can find Superhero Mandroid first; the hidden lab remains secure against low-level hacking; the inventor is alive but now exposed as a likely future target. - New characters: Superhero Mandroid, The Inventor, Pixel Demon. - New settings: Superhero Mandroid's Hidden Lab. - New key items: Rechargeable Holo Shield, Regenerating Rocket Fists. - Reference images added: superhero-mandroid-character.png , inventor-character.png , pixel-demon-character.png , hidden-lab-setting.png , rechargeable-holo-shield-key-item.png , regenerating-rocket-fists-key-item.png . Issue 2: Monsters Beneath Our Streets - Folder: series/superhero-mandroid/issues/issue-02/ - Logline: When a purple shutdown sludge creature threatens the city power plant, Superhero Mandroid saves the city by redirecting the sky monster into the diseased fatbergs beneath the streets. - Plot summary: A portal opens above the city, and Superhero Mandroid launches from his hidden lab through the retractable skylight using his rocket fists. A purple sludge creature leaks from the portal and shuts down every electronic device it touches, making direct contact too dangerous for an android hero. The inventor warns by holo message that the monster will reach the city power plant unless Mandroid acts fast. Mandroid blocks the sludge with an oversized holo-shield dam, but the creature drains the shield's charge. Remembering a documentary about sewer fatbergs, he uses rocket fists to pulverize a moat into the street. When the shield dies, the sludge drains into the sewers, where diseased fatbergs decay it away. - Continuity changes: The hidden lab has a retractable launch skylight; Superhero Mandroid learns that some portal monsters can shut down electronics and drain holo-shield energy; the city sewers contain dangerous fatberg colonies that can also defend the city under the right conditions. - New characters: Shutdown Sludge Creature, Sewer Fatbergs. - New settings: Mandroid's City, City Power Plant, City Sewers. - New key items: None. - Reference images added: shutdown-sludge-creature-character.png , sewer-fatbergs-character.png , mandroids-city-setting.png , city-power-plant-setting.png , city-sewers-setting.png . Issue 3: The Voice That Broke The Hall - Folder: series/superhero-mandroid/issues/issue-03/ - Logline: Superhero Mandroid battles a parasite-controlled opera singer whose amplified voice turns the opera house itself into a weapon. - Plot summary: A portal opens inside the Grand Opera House and releases a brain parasite that clamps onto an opera singer's head like a living helmet. Under the parasite's control, the singer attacks the crowd with supernatural sonic blasts. Superhero Mandroid punches through the back wall of the theater, but the possessed singer uses her amplified voice to rocket over him, smash him into the seating area, and pin him down. The inventor warns that the parasite is amplifying the singer's natural vocal power. Mandroid tries to advance behind his holo shield, but the blast drains the shield before he can get close. He abandons the close-range plan and fires a rocket fist through the opposing sonic force, knocking the parasite from her head and freeing her. - Continuity changes: Superhero Mandroid learns that some interdimensional demons can control human minds and weaponize a host's existing abilities; the Grand Opera House is damaged by sonic blasts and rocket impacts; the opera singer survives and returns to normal. - New characters: Opera Singer, Brain Parasite. - New settings: Grand Opera House. - New key items: None. - Reference images added: opera-singer-character.png , brain-parasite-character.png , grand-opera-house-setting.png .
