# Character And Reference Bible

## The Narrator

- Role: first-person witness, myth-maker, and emotional anchor of the true-story-based account.
- Age range: adult.
- Visual identifiers: medium height, expressive face, short dark hair mostly tucked under a blue hooded jacket, dark pants, white sneakers, and a small weathered notebook or phone used to record the sky myth.
- Continuity notes: keep the blue hooded jacket and notebook or phone consistent. The narrator can be shown from behind, in profile, or in close-up to keep the first-person feeling broad and relatable. Expressions move from wonder to fear, then calm awe, then unresolved curiosity.

## Loved Ones

- Role: the people whose safety makes the storm encounter emotionally decisive.
- Visual identifiers: two or three warm home-lit figures seen through doorways, windows, or morning interiors. Keep them gentle and grounded, with simple casual clothing and relieved body language.
- Continuity notes: they do not need names or individualized dialogue. Their safety is shown visually through an intact home, lit rooms, working power, and close protective gestures.

## Friends

- Role: witnesses to the second appearance during the Aurora Borealis.
- Visual identifiers: small group of three friends in casual jackets or hoodies, standing outdoors at night with phones lowered, faces lit by green, violet, and cyan aurora glow.
- Continuity notes: keep them visually secondary to the narrator and the sky eyes. Their shared stillness confirms that the return is witnessed by more than one person.

## The Sky Watchers

- Role: mysterious celestial guardians or omens.
- Visual identifiers: two colossal brilliant glowing eyes formed from storm light, cloud breaks, lightning, aurora ribbons, and star-like highlights. The eyes should feel benevolent but unreadable: radiant white-gold pupils, cyan and violet halos, and enormous scale above the city.
- Continuity notes: the eyes appear first in the tornado storm clouds and later inside the Aurora Borealis. They should match in shape and glow across both appearances, while the surrounding weather changes.

## The Tornado And Horizon

- Role: natural disaster and mythic gateway.
- Visual identifiers: a huge dark funnel tearing through neighborhoods, diagonal sheets of rain, flying debris, power flashes in the distance, siren-like red light accents, and an impossible horizon shifting through saturated cyan, magenta, gold, emerald, and violet.
- Continuity notes: make the tornado frightening and destructive without gore. Emphasize scale, motion, and neighborhood danger while keeping the narrator's home intact by the morning page.

## Visual Rules

- Full modern anime comic pages in 4:5 portrait aspect ratio.
- Use high saturation, high detail sharpness, bright glow, dynamic panel layouts, luminous weather, crisp linework, and cinematic color contrast.
- Caption boxes only; no speech bubbles, no thought bubbles, no floating dialogue.
- All readable story text must be generated directly inside the page art.
- Include the true-story basis in the generated page text, preferably on the cover and opening page.
