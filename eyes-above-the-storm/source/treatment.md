# Treatment

## Title

Eyes Above the Storm

## Logline

In a comic based on a true story, a narrator invents a myth of heroic sky watchers to soften humanity's fear of chaotic weather, then sees that myth appear during a devastating tornado and return days later inside the Aurora Borealis.

## Central Conflict

The narrator wants storms to feel less like meaningless destruction and more like part of a living, mysterious world. When a real tornado threatens their city, that invented wonder is tested by fear, destruction, and the question of whether the glowing eyes in the sky are imagination, coincidence, or protection.

## Theme

Myths can be invented, but sometimes belief becomes a lens that helps people survive what they cannot control.

## Ending

After the narrator's home and loved ones are spared, the glowing eyes return in the northern lights before the narrator and friends. The story ends unresolved but transformed: the sky watchers may be guardians, omens, or something stranger, and the narrator can no longer treat the myth as only a story.

## Emotional Arc

The narrator begins playful and imaginative, crafting a heroic legend to bring wonder into dangerous weather. The tornado turns that wonder into genuine fear, then the glowing eyes and untouched home create awe and gratitude. The Aurora return shifts the narrator from relief to mystery, ending with reverence instead of certainty.

## Cast

- The narrator: first-person witness and myth-maker, visually represented as an adult in a blue hooded jacket, dark pants, and white sneakers, often seen from behind or in expressive close-up so readers can inhabit the viewpoint.
- Loved ones: the narrator's family at home during the storm, shown as warm silhouettes and worried figures rather than individualized named characters.
- Friends: a small group who watch the Aurora Borealis with the narrator several days later.
- The sky watchers: two massive brilliant glowing eyes in the clouds and northern lights, cosmic guardians whose motives remain unknown.
- The storm: the destructive tornado and color-shifting horizon, treated as both natural disaster and mythic threshold.

## Page Count

Eight pages total: cover plus seven story pages.

## Art Direction

Modern anime comic aesthetic instead of the repo's normal realistic photo style: high color saturation, crisp sharp detail, bright glow effects, dynamic paneling, luminous weather, expressive sky color, cinematic motion, clean rectangular caption boxes, and no speech bubbles.
