Kaelus the Archmage stood alone in the center of a shattered stone arena, his long midnight-blue coat stirring in air that should have been still. Silver runes glowed across its sleeves, matching the pale light in his sharp gray eyes. Once a royal battle-mage, Kaelus had abandoned the court after exposing a conspiracy among its ruling sorcerers, and since then he had survived as a wandering protector of people hunted by magical powers. He knew someone had finally answered his defiance with an assassin. Somewhere in the chamber, an Invisible Stalker circled him—a towering humanoid shape made from compressed wind, summoned from the Plane of Air and bound by magic to pursue one target until either hunter or quarry was dead. It had no hatred of its own, only an unbreakable command: kill Kaelus.

Kaelus whispered an incantation and cast See Invisibility. His vision sharpened, and the empty air thirty-five feet away suddenly revealed a twisting figure of storm and dust, half-hidden behind a broken pillar. The Stalker had been preparing to strike. It surged forward with fists like battering rams, but Kaelus reacted instantly, punching toward it as a translucent barrier of blue energy burst from his knuckles. Both blows crashed against the Shield spell and scattered harmlessly across its surface. The Stalker recoiled, startled that its greatest advantage had vanished, while Kaelus planted his feet and gathered freezing mana between his palms.

Holding his hands like a warrior charging a legendary energy blast, Kaelus stared directly into the creature’s swirling face. “I am sorry you must die by my hand,” he said, “but I have no choice except to defend myself.” Frost poured around his arms as he thrust them forward and shouted, “Terrible Tundra!” A sixty-foot cone of supernatural cold exploded through the arena. Ice raced across the floor and pillars, and the Stalker’s invisible body became trapped inside a jagged outline of frost. The blast wounded it badly, but the elemental remained upright. Through cracking ice, it rasped, “Your death was purchased, Archmage.”

The Stalker charged again, driven by the spell that enslaved it. Kaelus blocked two more attacks with another projected Shield, then faced the wounded creature with grim resolve. “You are compelled to fight until the end,” he said. “Speak now and reveal what you know, and I will make your death quick.” The threat broke through the Stalker’s discipline. It confessed that it had never seen its summoner; its orders had arrived through a silver mask engraved with a closed eye. The hidden voice had named Kaelus “the Last Witness.” The revelation confirmed that the conspiracy from his past had not forgotten him, but the Stalker still raised its fists. It could reveal the truth, yet it could not disobey its command.

Kaelus clasped his hands into a precise magical sign. Mana rushed through his body like fire, and he evaporated into a streak of glowing smoke. Misty Step carried him thirty feet backward, and he immediately sprinted another thirty, placing the length of the arena between himself and his enemy. The Stalker raced after him with supernatural speed. It flew fifty feet in an instant and spent the last of its momentum closing the remaining distance, unable to attack. Kaelus had planned for exactly that. With the elemental lined up before him, he folded his thumbs inward, opened his palms, and shouted, “Titanic Tundra: Blizzard Bombardment!” A barrage of icy spheres and radiant mana bolts hammered the Stalker, leaving its storm-body cracked and barely intact.

Still alive, the assassin struck back with desperate fury. Its first fist broke through Kaelus’s defense and slammed into his ribs, nearly throwing him from his feet. Kaelus spun with the impact, transformed the motion into a counterpunch, and projected his final Shield directly into the second attack. Wind and arcane force collided with an explosive crack. The blow failed, but Kaelus knew his protective magic was almost exhausted. The Stalker hovered before him, its frozen body unraveling, yet the binding command continued forcing it forward. Neither combatant could retreat. Only one final exchange remained.

Kaelus dropped to one knee, braced his forearm, and aimed his palm upward beneath the charging elemental. “Diagonal Draft: Rising Glacier!” he roared. A colossal pillar of ice, snow, and concentrated mana erupted diagonally from his hand. It caught the Stalker at point-blank range and launched it toward the ceiling, freezing its violent winds solid as it rose. Cracks spread through the suspended figure until it shattered in a brilliant storm of ice and silver light. The arena fell silent, and a fragment of metal struck the floor beside Kaelus: part of a silver mask bearing the image of a closed eye. The Stalker was dead, its forced mission finally ended, but Kaelus now possessed proof of the enemy behind it. He picked up the fragment and walked from the arena alive, no longer merely surviving an assassination, but ready to hunt the conspirators who had ordered it.
