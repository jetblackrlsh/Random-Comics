Ruby Sun: The Power We Share

Ruby Sun grows up in her father's neighborhood dojo, where his most important lesson is that anyone can be knocked down and strength begins with learning how to rise again. When the local mob escalates an extortion dispute from fists to guns, her father dies protecting the dojo, proving that even a brilliant martial artist can become a single point of failure.

Orphaned Ruby survives as a flashy street martial-arts performer. Curious neighbors ask her to teach them, and her first students eventually give her a home. Their own persecution by the mob reawakens Ruby's fear that skill cannot stop every weapon. Refusing to buy a gun, she climbs the mountain for which her father's dojo was named and discovers his old master: an elderly pink-furred humanoid monkey who controls golden fire.

The master offers Ruby a pair of futuristic golden bands that her father once refused. They let their wearer assume a pink-furred, monkey-like form with a tail, tremendous speed and agility, and a blazing golden aura. Ruby accepts them but refuses to let supernatural force replace discipline. The master warns that weapons and technology will keep evolving; Ruby counters that power is useless if its possessor hides on a mountain. Their argument ends in mutual respect, and Ruby returns to the city.

Ruby opens a new dojo and teaches store owners, workers, teenagers, parents, and delivery drivers to protect one another. When ordinary technique is not enough, she uses the bands, becoming the flamboyant pink-furred heroine of stories that make the mob afraid. Mob leader Vincent Vale decides the bands are a shortcut to everything Ruby earned through training.

Vale raids the dojo and traps a college-age student in an office. Ruby surrenders the bands without hesitation. Vale transforms and becomes faster and stronger than Ruby, but he has no balance, control, judgment, or skill. Ruby studies those weaknesses, remembers being six years old beneath the dojo ceiling, and prepares to rise again.

Ruby cannot overpower Vale alone. Her students join her, free hostages, protect the vulnerable, and attack as a coordinated community. Together they redirect Vale's strength until Ruby and the whole dojo throw him onto his back and the bands fly free. Ruby realizes that neither martial arts nor supernatural power is the complete answer. Her true power is the network of people she taught to stand together.

Years later, Ruby's students have become teachers across the city. The mob can threaten one store only to face ten neighboring stores beside it. Ruby still wears the bands and trains as if she might lose them tomorrow, but she is no longer the city's single point of failure. When a new student falls in her dojo, she offers a hand and repeats the word that carried her from frightened child to community leader: "Again."
