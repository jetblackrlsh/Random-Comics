# Treatment

## Title

Ruby Sun: The Power We Share

## Publication Date

2026-08-26

## Format

Standalone Random Comics one-shot, thirteen pages total: one cover and twelve story pages.

## Page-Count Rationale

Seven story pages would force the childhood, mountain training, community campaign, and climactic collective fight to compete for space. Twelve story pages give every major change a visible cause and consequence, support long explanatory captions without shrinking them into unreadable blocks, and preserve the opening and closing "Again" as a deliberate emotional bookend.

## Logline

After the mob kills her martial-artist father, Ruby Sun inherits golden bands that grant overwhelming supernatural power—but when a mob boss steals them, Ruby proves that neither weapons nor one exceptional fighter can protect a city as completely as ordinary people trained to stand together.

## Central Conflict

Ruby must decide what truly makes her powerful: the martial arts her father taught her, the supernatural bands his master gives her, or the community she builds. Vincent Vale believes power can be stolen and worn. Ruby learns that durable power must be distributed.

## Theme

Real protection is shared capacity. Skill matters. Adaptable technology matters. Neither should turn one hero into a community's single point of failure.

## Ending

Ruby and her students defeat Vale together after he steals the bands. Years later, those students have become teachers throughout the city. Ruby still trains and transforms when necessary, but the mob can no longer make everyone helpless by defeating one woman. The story closes with Ruby helping a fallen beginner rise and saying, "Again."

## Emotional Arc

Childhood frustration becomes traumatic loss; survival becomes belonging; fear becomes a search for greater power; power becomes responsibility; solitary heroism becomes collective confidence; Ruby's old lesson about rising becomes a citywide practice.

## Cast

- Ruby Sun: cocky, theatrical, warm-hearted Asian martial artist who becomes a community organizer and supernatural protector.
- Ruby's father: disciplined neighborhood dojo teacher who believes skill and restraint are sufficient until the mob escalates to guns.
- The old master: pink-furred humanoid monkey martial artist who taught Ruby's father and lends Ruby his golden power through the bands.
- Vincent Vale: mob leader who delegates violence and believes the bands are a shortcut to earned strength.
- Ruby's students and neighbors: diverse ordinary citizens who become the decisive force in the climax and the lasting heroes of the city.

## Art Direction

Realistic candid superhero photo-comic with documentary phone-photo or handheld photojournalism panels, believable urban locations, natural imperfect framing, and photoreal golden-fire VFX. Ruby's ordinary and transformed states must remain unmistakably the same person. Use roomy rectangular caption boxes only, no speech bubbles or thought bubbles, no page numbers, and no captions added after generation. The action should remain spatially obvious from panel to panel.
