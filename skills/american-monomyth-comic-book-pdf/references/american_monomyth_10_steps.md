## Beat Guidance

### Pages 2-3: The Edenic Community

- Page 2 establishes the setting with a strong opening image.
- Page 3 focuses on the people, values, or way of life that must be protected.
- Use bright, appealing color and clear environmental storytelling.
- Page-turn hook: a strange sign of danger appears.

### Pages 4-5: Evil Threatens the Community

- Page 4 reveals the first attack, strange event, or villainous action.
- Page 5 escalates the danger and proves it is not isolated.
- Shift the palette toward intense danger while preserving vivid color.
- Page-turn hook: the threat proves stronger than expected.

### Pages 6-7: Ordinary Institutions Fail

- Page 6 shows the official or normal response.
- Page 7 shows that response breaking down.
- Institutions may be outmatched, too slow, divided, corrupt, or unprepared; they do not have to be stupid.
- Page-turn hook: someone realizes only an extraordinary figure can help.

### Pages 8-9: The Community Becomes Helpless

- Page 8 focuses on civilians in danger or a city falling apart.
- Page 9 creates a moment where hope seems almost gone.
- Use close-ups, frightened eyes, reaching hands, protective embraces, and scale contrast.
- Page-turn hook: the hero appears, awakens, arrives, or is called.

### Pages 10-11: The Exceptional Hero Appears

- Page 10 gives the hero an iconic reveal.
- Page 11 clarifies the hero's powers, mission, code, wound, burden, or unusual relationship to the community.
- Use strong silhouette, heroic perspective, clear costume design, and glowing power effects.
- Page-turn hook: the hero enters the danger zone.

### Pages 12-13: The Hero Resists Ordinary Limitations

- Page 12 shows the hero overcoming obstacles that stopped everyone else.
- Page 13 shows progress and the emotional cost of being exceptional.
- Use dynamic action panels, speed lines, energy trails, shields, impact effects, and isolation.
- Page-turn hook: the hero discovers the true source of the threat.

### Pages 14-15: Redemptive Force Confronts Evil

- Page 14 begins the major confrontation.
- Page 15 intensifies it and forces a defining choice.
- The hero should win through the story's central value, not only raw strength.
- Page-turn hook: victory requires a personal cost.

### Pages 16-17: The Community Is Restored

- Page 16 shows the immediate aftermath: portals closing, fires going out, civilians saved, or the villain falling.
- Page 17 shows public relief, stunned silence, gratitude, fear, or uneasy recognition.
- Shift back toward warmth, clarity, and open space.
- Page-turn hook: the hero realizes they do not truly belong in the restored world.

### Pages 18-19: The Hero Does Not Fully Join the Community

- Page 18 shows the community's response to the hero.
- Page 19 shows the hero's emotional response.
- Use framing, distance, rooftops, glass, portals, crowds, or contrasting colored light to show separation.
- Page-turn hook: the hero turns away from the community.

### Pages 20-21: The Hero Withdraws or Waits for the Next Crisis

- Page 20 shows the hero's withdrawal.
- Page 21 gives the final story image.
- The ending may be triumphant, lonely, peaceful, bittersweet, mysterious, rebellious, tragic, or unsettling.
- Final hook: the hero disappears, transforms, watches, or begins a new path.

### Page 22: Poster-Style Back Cover Summary

- Make the back cover a designed poster generated directly by `image_gen`.
- Include integrated poster text only if it can be rendered as part of the image: a bold tagline, compact summary, theme statement, hero quote, or issue moral.
- Use a central hero image, key symbols, villain or threat motifs, and three to five visual beat fragments when useful.
- Do not use PDF tooling, HTML/CSS, canvas, or later image editing to add back-cover text.
