# Page Script

Every page is a full realistic candid photo-comic page in 4:5 portrait aspect ratio. All readable title and story text must be generated directly inside clean rectangular caption boxes in the page art. Use caption boxes only; no speech bubbles, no thought bubbles, no floating subtitles, no blank text boxes, no later text overlay, no page numbers, no panel numbers, no caption labels, and no duplicated caption wording on the same page.

## Page 1: Cover

- Story role: Introduce Clifford and Angora as an unlikely fantasy duo and foreshadow their tavern, library, and river mishaps.
- Layout: Full-page cinematic ensemble cover. Angora stands in the lower foreground with shield raised and pink-violet psychic sword blazing. Clifford towers behind her, smiling as green-gold music magic curls from his ornate lute. Warm tavern light fills the upper-left background, blue magical library shelves fill the upper-right, and a river explosion with fleeing shark bandits appears along the bottom edge.
- Readable title text: "CLIFFORD AND ANGORA'S FIRST ADVENTURE"
- Visual beats: Match the supplied reference designs closely in realistic live-action fantasy form; make their friendship and contrasting powers immediately clear.

## Page 2: A Song For A Fighter

- Story role: Establish both protagonists, Angora's goal, Clifford's offer, and the harmless summon that takes offense.
- Layout: Four large rectangular panels in clear top-to-bottom reading order.
- Caption boxes: "Inside a lively tavern, Clifford looked like a mighty goliath warrior—but he thought of himself as a musician, not a fighter." "Angora entered seeking a worthy opponent who could test her skill with shield and psychic sword." "Clifford offered to summon a creature with his magical lute, hoping his music could give the elven fighter the challenge she wanted." "The creature arrived friendly and confused. When Angora offered friendship, it snapped that it already had friends—and called them all at once."
- Visual beats: Clifford among normal-sized patrons; Angora enters and introduces herself; Clifford plays luminous notes; the harmless green horned visitor appears, looks indignant, and raises one hand as portals begin opening.

## Page 3: The Reversed Holy Song

- Story role: Escalate the tavern accident, reveal Clifford's musical mistake, and show Angora protect him.
- Layout: Four cinematic panels, with the final sword display the largest.
- Caption boxes: "Twelve tiefling women burst into the tavern ready to brawl, and Clifford realized his attempt to help had made everything worse." "He played what he believed was a holy song, hoping divine power would drive them away." "The tune caused no pain. It was a reversed version of tiefling sacred music, and they heard Clifford's performance as a terrible insult to their culture." "While outrage distracted them, Angora flooded her psychic sword with brilliant light. The display frightened every tiefling out of the tavern."
- Visual beats: twelve varied tiefling brawlers fill the room; Clifford plays urgently; tieflings point and shout in cultural outrage while he realizes the mistake; Angora plants her shield and ignites a huge controlled pink-violet blade flare as the crowd retreats.

## Page 4: Lessons Of Heart And Mind

- Story role: Move the new companions to the magical library and give each the lesson that will matter later.
- Layout: Four large panels with blue-violet library atmosphere and clear spatial continuity.
- Caption boxes: "Grateful for Angora's protection, Clifford admitted that he needed to understand the magical music he played." "They traveled together to a magical library, where Angora found a warrior's text teaching that the mind—not the body—was the deepest source of fighting power." "Clifford discovered the Tune of the Astral Blade, a song said to strengthen a magical sword." "Certain he had finally found the perfect way to help, Clifford invited Angora to test the tune after they rested beside the nearby river."
- Visual beats: pair leave tavern as friends; Angora reads a glowing warrior book; Clifford searches floating music manuscripts; both lean over the discovered astral score with hopeful smiles before departing.

## Page 5: The Tune That Weakened The Blade

- Story role: Begin the river ambush, make the shark bandits' motive obvious, and turn Clifford's promised solution into the low point.
- Layout: Four action panels, ending on the flickering sword and encircling raiders.
- Caption boxes: "Before Clifford and Angora could rest beside the river, six humanoid shark bandits surrounded them." "The raiders wanted Angora's glowing psychic sword and rushed to overpower her before she could escape." "Clifford played the Tune of the Astral Blade, expecting its magic to make her weapon stronger." "Instead, the pink-violet blade flickered and weakened. With the bandits closing in, the two new companions rapidly lost ground."
- Visual beats: quiet river arrival interrupted by sharks rising from shallows; sharks point greedily at sword; Angora blocks spears while Clifford plays; sword becomes a weak unstable glow and the surrounded pair look alarmed.

## Page 6: Power In The Mind

- Story role: Let Clifford's failure accidentally create an opening and show Angora choose the library's mental lesson.
- Layout: Four tense panels, from comic frustration to concentrated psychic power.
- Caption boxes: "Clifford cleared his throat and attempted the astral tune again. The sword still refused to strengthen." "His performance sounded so unpleasant that the furious shark bandits turned away from Angora and charged the bard instead." "That accidental distraction gave Angora one precious moment to remember the library's lesson." "She stopped relying only on physical strength, focused upon the power inside her mind, and launched a psychic blast from the sword."
- Visual beats: Clifford winces through a wrong chord; irritated sharks cover their ears and pivot toward him; Angora closes her eyes behind her shield while magenta energy gathers; she opens glowing eyes and fires a massive psychic bolt past the bandits.

## Page 7: The Blast That Missed

- Story role: Resolve the external danger through Angora's spectacular miss and the bandits' fearful misunderstanding.
- Layout: Three large panels to give the river explosion maximum scale.
- Caption boxes: "Angora's psychic blast missed every shark bandit." "It struck the river instead, releasing a tremendous explosion that scattered water into the sky and briefly opened a crater in the rushing current." "Convinced they had challenged opponents far more dangerous than expected, the terrified bandits abandoned the sword and fled."
- Visual beats: magenta bolt passes between astonished sharks; enormous photoreal blue-white water blast erupts with a temporary hollow in the river; soaked shark bandits scramble downstream and into the woods while Clifford and Angora stare at the result.

## Page 8: Heart And Mind

- Story role: Give emotional closure and define the friendship that emerged from their imperfect victory.
- Layout: Four quiet cinematic panels moving from apology to a shared departure along the river.
- Caption boxes: "With the danger gone, Clifford apologized. ‘I think I need music lessons so I can become better at this.’" "Angora answered without blame: ‘That's okay. The music is in your heart.’" "Clifford smiled and returned the encouragement: ‘And you're the best fighter because your power is in your mind.’" "Their first adventure ended without a perfect spell or decisive strike, but with two unlikely companions recognizing the strength within one another."
- Visual beats: wet embarrassed Clifford lowers lute; Angora rests the dimmed sword and smiles; close two-shot as they exchange encouragement; wide sunset departure with Clifford and Angora walking side by side along the riverbank, lute, sword, and shield visible.
