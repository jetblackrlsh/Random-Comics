# Treatment

## Title

Clifford and Angora's First Adventure

## Format

Standalone one-shot, eight pages total: one cover and seven story pages.

## Logline

When Clifford's unreliable magical music repeatedly creates trouble, the gentle goliath bard and the battle-hungry elf Angora survive angry tieflings and shark bandits by discovering that their real strength lies not in perfect technique, but in the heart and mind they encourage in each other.

## Central Conflict

Clifford wants to help Angora prove herself as a warrior, but every song he plays has an unexpected effect: a harmless summon calls hostile friends, a holy tune insults tiefling culture, and a sword-enhancing melody weakens Angora's psychic blade. Angora must learn to trust her mind when neither muscle nor Clifford's magic works, while Clifford must accept that his value as a companion is greater than his mistakes.

## World Logic

Clifford's ornate lute channels magic through learned songs, but inaccurate performance or incomplete musical knowledge can reverse, distort, or culturally misapply a spell. Angora's sword is psychic rather than purely physical: its pink-violet energy answers to focus, conviction, and mental discipline. The magical library contains practical spell music and philosophical warrior texts, while humanoid peoples such as elves, tieflings, goliaths, and shark folk share the surrounding fantasy realm.

## Theme

Power becomes reliable when it is grounded in self-knowledge and mutual encouragement. Clifford's music comes from his heart; Angora's strength comes from her mind.

## Ending

Angora's missed psychic blast detonates in the river and frightens the shark bandits away. Clifford apologizes for his failed music, but Angora assures him that the music is in his heart. He returns the reassurance by telling her that her greatest fighting power is in her mind. They leave the river as true companions.

## Emotional Arc

Angora begins eager to prove herself through combat, while Clifford wants to help despite doubting his ability as a fighter. Their first tavern disaster shows Angora's courage and Clifford's good intentions. The library gives each of them a new path, but the river ambush turns those lessons into a crisis when Clifford's tune fails. Angora succeeds only after internalizing the lesson of mental strength. Instead of blaming one another, they recognize the genuine gift inside each friend's imperfect efforts.

## Cast

- Clifford: towering Black goliath bard, kindhearted musician, and unreliable novice at magical battle songs.
- Angora: red-haired elf fighter carrying a wooden shield and a pink-violet psychic sword.
- The summoned visitor: harmless creature whose wounded pride calls twelve tiefling friends.
- Twelve tiefling women: aggressive tavern brawlers angered by Clifford's reversed sacred song.
- Shark bandits: humanoid river raiders who try to steal Angora's psychic sword.

## Art Direction

Realistic candid photo-comic with rich high-fantasy production design inspired by the supplied character reference. The tavern uses warm amber practical light; the magical library shifts to cool moon-blue and violet illumination; the river battle uses natural daylight, rushing water, and photoreal magical impacts. Clifford and Angora must remain visually consistent across all pages. Use handheld fantasy photojournalism, believable costumes and props, clean rectangular caption boxes, and physically convincing green-gold music magic and pink-violet psychic energy. No speech bubbles or thought bubbles.
