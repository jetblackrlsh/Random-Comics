# Manager Of Monsters

## Logline

A mall store manager gets lost in an impossible storage room where monsters obey workplace rules, only to become their king and fall when he forgets the ordinary life that gave him power.

## Central Conflict

Evan Rook survives the hidden room by recognizing monstrous distortions of people from his mall job and using mundane authority against them. Every victory takes him farther from the store and deeper into the room's logic, until the memories that made him powerful fade and leave him defenseless against a monster that looks too much like his former self.

## Theme

Authority built on habit is fragile. Evan controls the impossible only while he remembers the small human routines behind it, and he loses himself when control becomes more important than the life he came from.

## Cast

- Evan Rook: A tired mall store manager in his late 30s, practical and overworked, whose ordinary memories become a survival tool.
- Marcy-Monster: A monstrous mirror of Evan's coworker Marcy, with fleshy headphones for ears and one smartphone-like eye.
- The Familiar Monsters: Warped reflections of mall coworkers, customers, vendors, security staff, and supervisors.
- Manager-Monster: A monstrous mirror of Evan himself, still wearing the old store name badge and carrying the plain exhaustion Evan has forgotten.

## Emotional Arc

Evan begins annoyed by another store problem and confident that every backroom has a map. Fear replaces confidence when the storage room expands beyond reason, but his first command over Marcy-Monster gives him a rush of control. As more monsters submit, he grows regal and detached. By the end, his forgotten humanity returns as an enemy he cannot name, and his kingdom collapses because it was only ever a distorted mirror of the world he abandoned.

## Ending

Manager-Monster attacks Evan after Evan fails to recognize himself. The other monsters abandon their king and join the attack. The final image shows the hidden storage world returned to its normal monstrous order: endless shelves, distorted worker-creatures, and an unlabeled door waiting somewhere beyond sight.
