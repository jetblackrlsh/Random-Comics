A manager working at a store in the mall discovers a hidden door that he has never seen before. The door is not on the store map and is oddly unlabeled. He walks through it and finds what first looks like an ordinary storage room. The deeper he explores, the stranger the objects become, and after losing track of time he realizes he can no longer see the door.

Shelves continue for impossible distances. A monstrous version of one of his coworkers emerges, with fleshy headphones for ears and a single smartphone-like eye. The manager recognizes just enough of the coworker to command the creature with mundane workplace threats, and it obeys. He keeps going, subduing other familiar monsters the same way. His memories of ordinary mall life become the key to controlling the strange room.

As time passes, those memories fade. The manager stops feeling like a mall employee and becomes the king of the monsters. When a monster version of himself finally appears, he cannot recognize it because it resembles the old life he has forgotten. Without memory to protect him, the monster version attacks. The other monsters side with their own kind, destroy the king, and the hidden world returns to normal.
