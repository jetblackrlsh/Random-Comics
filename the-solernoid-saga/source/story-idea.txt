The Solernoid Saga: A Story of The Spectrum

The Spectrum is a tidally locked world divided between an eternal sunward face, a permanent nightward face, and a narrow twilight band. Phototrophs—humanoid fungal beings sustained by sunlight—dominate the sunward fungal jungles. Vampires rule much of the darkness and depend on human blood. Humans live mainly in the twilight band, where scarce farmland and livestock force every village into different compromises.

Some humans eat fungus and fight phototrophs. Others refuse fungal food and ally with phototroph communities, joining an underground mycelial communications network. Some villages bargain with vampires for defense; others hunt them. Humushes are human-phototroph hybrids. Daywalkers are human-vampire hybrids with vampiric strength and hunger but greater tolerance for sunlight.

Brightwalk is a human twilight settlement that harvests fungal jungle and accepts protection from a neighboring nightward vampire lord. Nearby, humans and phototrophs coexist in a settlement called the Network, an important communications hub. The three communities already distrust one another when an escape pod arrives from a doomed world called Solernoid.

Solernoid's people can absorb sunlight like living batteries and shape captured solar power through technology. One family launches its infant in a pod that feeds him solar radiation during the journey. The pod crashes in vampire territory, radiating so much solar energy that the vampires cannot approach. When the energy fades and the infant stops glowing, the vampire lord gives him to human parents in Brightwalk but keeps the pod hidden.

Experimentation with the pod inspires the vampires to create Hardlight: stored solar power projected as weapons, shields, restraints, and cages. Solar cloaks let vampires endure daylight while charging their tools. Brightwalk knows them as protectors; the Network knows them as raiders.

When phototrophs learn to tear away solar cloaks, the lord equips Brightwalk's daywalkers as elite frontline soldiers. They can wield Hardlight beneath the sun without protection. The adopted boy grows up idolizing Silverfang and the other daywalker heroes.

During adolescence, his solar nature awakens. He charges Hardlight equipment merely by standing nearby, then develops immense strength, durability, enormous leaps, and devastating eye beams. Every beam consumes his stored energy, leaving him dark and weak until he recharges, so Silverfang and the daywalkers protect him whenever his light runs low.

Phototroph thinkers know nothing about Solernoid or the pod. They mistake Brightwalk's champion for a rare advanced humush and begin deliberately pairing humans with bioluminescent foxfire phototrophs. Selected luminous offspring are trained from childhood as anti-Hardlight soldiers called Firefoxes.

When the Firefoxes attack Brightwalk, their light-based power can hurt the Solernoid through force and heat, but it also replenishes him. He learns that the Network considers him a monster allied with human-hunting vampires. Having heard only Brightwalk's history, he decides the mycelial network is spreading propaganda and that peace requires destroying it.

He attacks the Network alone. Humans, phototroph warriors, cordyceps controllers, armored fungal giants, slime-mold fighters, and Firefoxes defend their home. Surrounded, he releases all his stored solar energy. The blast devastates the battlefield and severs major underground mycelial pathways.

The communications silence causes distant villages to send defenders toward emergencies that do not exist. Vampire clans exploit the empty garrisons and capture large numbers of humans in raids remembered as the Taking. The Solernoid, exhausted and nearly dark, sees ordinary Network humans still staring at him with terror and realizes their fear was never merely propaganda.

He returns to celebration. The vampires feast, nearby phototroph forces are broken, sacrifices demanded from Brightwalk temporarily decrease, and Brightwalk is safer than it has been in generations. Yet he cannot forget the suffering his decision caused. He still never learns his true origin, but Silverfang helps him understand that identity can begin with the people who choose and protect one another.

Years later, the Solernoid and Silverfang stand with a child of their own, descended from an alien solar being and a daywalker born between human and vampire. The future of The Spectrum remains unresolved, but the Solernoid finally knows that he has a family.
